# Panel Notes — Leo’s Flooring

## WHY critique

**Steve Jobs — 8.0**
- The hero finally feels like the product: wood, weight, finish, trust.
- Keep the first screen focused on confidence and call action. Don’t drift into contractor brochure language.

**Paul Graham — 7.8**
- The page is clearest when it states facts plainly: license, consultation, services, timeline.
- Cut phrases that sound like the existing site’s “canvas of your home” copy.

**David Ogilvy — 8.1**
- “Floors that can take real life” is specific enough to sell durability without sounding fake.
- Phone and free consultation are strong direct-response hooks. Keep both early.

**WHY average:** 7.97

**Next changes**
1. Move quick facts immediately under the hero.
2. Make CSLB/license language visible without turning it into a stats strip.
3. Keep the headline practical, not poetic.
4. Use the official wood photos as proof, not decoration.

## WHAT critique

**Don Norman — 8.1**
- The page answers the homeowner’s first questions quickly: what services, where, how to start.
- The service grid works because each card says one thing.

**Steve Krug — 8.2**
- Phone, hours, base, and service area are easy to scan.
- Estimate path is obvious. Keep CTA labels matched to actions.

**Jakob Nielsen — 7.9**
- Good recognition over recall: service names match how customers search.
- Address source needs care. If a confident Google business pin is unavailable, don’t add a generic map link.

**WHAT average:** 8.07

**Next changes**
1. Add a concise process section before the final estimate CTA.
2. Avoid a map link unless it’s a named business listing.
3. Keep body copy under 2-3 sentences per block.
4. Preserve mobile hamburger from v0.

## HOW critique

**Massimo Vignelli — 8.1**
- Strong grid discipline. The page has one material system and one accent.
- Typography has enough contrast without becoming precious.

**Erik Spiekermann — 8.0**
- Archivo is a good contractor face here: direct, sturdy, legible.
- Libre Baskerville italic gives the wood warmth without making the business feel like a boutique hotel.

**Dieter Rams — 8.0**
- No stats bar. Good. The proof belongs in sentences and cards.
- Remove anything that explains the redesign rationale to the visitor. Sources file can carry that weight.

**Emil Kowalski — 8.4**
- Use standard reveal motion only. The surface photos don’t need hover zoom or tricks.
- Button active scale and reduced-motion support are enough.

**HOW average:** 8.13

**Next changes**
1. Add restrained reveal/stagger motion.
2. Keep image interactions static.
3. Use dark gallery as the main visual chapter.
4. Do final copy pass for AI tells and source-rationale language.
