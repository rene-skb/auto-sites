# Panel Notes - California Electrical Services

## WHY critique - v0

### Steve Jobs - 8.2
- The hero has a point of view. “Power for plants that can’t blink” feels built for industrial buyers, not homeowners.
- The project proof is where the site becomes inevitable. The names and numbers make CES feel serious.
- The right-side hero card is useful but visually loud. Let the headline own the stage.

### Paul Graham - 7.8
- The best copy is specific: license, project names, amperage, UPS, Starline busway, 35,000 feet of cable.
- Some lines talk about the demo instead of the business. That breaks trust immediately.
- Cut the explanatory scaffolding and keep the plain facts.

### David Ogilvy - 8.1
- The headline sells the business benefit: uptime for facilities.
- The project section is stronger than generic service claims because it shows proof.
- Contact should feel like a project quote path, not a soft brochure ending.

**WHY average:** 8.03

**Changes for v1:**
1. Remove all visible “demo / redesign / this version” language.
2. Reduce the hero spec card’s visual weight so the headline stays dominant.
3. Tighten the intro around CES capability, not our redesign choices.
4. Make the contact CTA sound like a project quote conversation.

## WHAT critique - v1

### Don Norman - 8.0
- Strong signifiers: headlines, CTAs, cards, and project proof make the page purpose clear quickly.
- “Design it. Build it. Keep it running.” explains the service lifecycle well.
- Some project rows feel like they might be clickable even though they are static proof.

### Steve Krug - 8.5
- The page answers what CES does, who they serve, why to trust them, and how to contact them in a logical order.
- “Power for plants that can’t blink” and “Bring the drawings. Start the quote.” are memorable and useful.
- The hero is large. A mid-page contact prompt after proof would help visitors who are ready before the final section.

### Jakob Nielsen - 7.5
- Scannability is strong because of short sections, named clients, metrics, and contact facts.
- Credibility is strong: license number, named clients, locations, quantities, map, phone, and email.
- Some small uppercase labels and white-on-image text need higher contrast on mobile.

**WHAT average:** 8.00

**Changes for v2:**
1. Add a mid-page CTA after project proof so ready buyers don’t need to reach the footer.
2. Increase contrast and size for small uppercase labels.
3. Tighten long body paragraphs where possible.
4. Make contact actions more button-like in the final section.

## HOW critique - v2

### Massimo Vignelli - 8.0
- The modular grid and section rhythm are disciplined; project rows and service cards feel orderly.
- The bold grotesk hierarchy is strong, but repeated oversized type starts to lose elegance.
- A few card effects and image overlays could be quieter for a more timeless system.

### Erik Spiekermann - 8.5
- The type system feels like a workhorse industrial brand: punchy headings, clear labels, readable body.
- Some dense headlines feel squeezed and need a touch more breathing room.
- The hero stat panel is useful, but the glass effect reduces readability.

### Dieter Rams - 7.5
- The page is useful and specific without decorative overload.
- Project proof and service specificity answer what CES does, where, and for whom.
- Rounded cards, dark overlays, and repeated large type could be quieter.

### Emil Kowalski - 8.0
- Motion should stay calm: reveal headlines, proof rows, and cards without spectacle.
- Avoid flashy image effects; industrial trust needs controlled movement.
- Give major blocks more breathing room so the scroll feels premium instead of heavy.

**HOW average:** 8.00

**Changes for v3/v4:**
1. Reduce repeated oversized headline scale after the hero.
2. Make the hero spec panel more readable and less glassy.
3. Quiet a few radius/effect choices without stripping personality.
4. Keep motion restrained: reveal, stagger, button press only.
