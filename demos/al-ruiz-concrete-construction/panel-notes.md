# Panel Notes — Al Ruiz Concrete Construction

## Creative direction

- Business: Al Ruiz Concrete Construction, concrete and masonry contractor serving Burbank, Glendale, Los Angeles County, and the San Fernando Valley.
- Source-backed proof: family/local ownership, in business since 1980, 40+ years of experience, free estimates, fully insured, CA State Contractor License #685-735, 10% first-customer offer.
- Direction: a contractor quote desk with big blocky type, clay/sand palette, official project images, and a phone-first conversion path. No fabricated reviews, no generic map link, no invented origin story.
- Rotation: hero pattern `centered-minimal / oversized contractor board` is different from last 3 builds; typography `Bebas Neue + IBM Plex Sans` is different from last 3; review/proof treatment and visit/contact structure are also different.

## WHY critique

### Steve Jobs — 7.8
- The large type has conviction, but the hero is a little abstract. Put the concrete work itself closer to the headline.
- The page starts to feel like an internal redesign case study when it says “official site” or “current site.” Customers should never see the scaffolding.
- The 10% section has stage presence. Tighten the call to action so it sells the phone call, not the discount graphic.

### Paul Graham — 7.6
- Cut the meta language. “Official project image,” “official guidance,” and “current site” are source notes, not page copy.
- “Old-school contractor proof, clear estimate path” sounds constructed. Say the useful thing plainly.
- The copy is strongest when it names real work: driveways, patios, retaining walls, grading.

### David Ogilvy — 8.0
- The offer is concrete and useful. Use it near a phone CTA.
- “Concrete that makes the outside usable” is good, but support it immediately with the service list.
- “Call for a free estimate” will pull better than “Call.”

**WHY average:** 7.80

**Changes for v1**
1. Remove source/meta language from visible copy and footer.
2. Make the hero subhead more service-specific.
3. Change CTAs to “Call for a free estimate” and make offer CTA phone-first.
4. Rewrite the intro headline in plain customer language.

## WHAT critique

### Don Norman — 8.0
- The service grouping helps a first-time visitor understand what Al Ruiz does.
- The phone path is clear, but callback should be described as a callback request, not a vague online action.
- Add one repeated estimate CTA after the service cards so visitors do not need to wait for the bottom contact section.

### Steve Krug — 7.9
- The page scans well because the big headings carry the structure.
- The proof line works, but license and free-estimate information should feel more actionable.
- Contact is far down the page. A mid-page “ready for an estimate?” strip would reduce friction.

### Jakob Nielsen — 7.8
- Avoid ambiguous “same-day service” copy. Keep “call for details” attached every time.
- Do not rely on tiny photo labels to explain services.
- Keep the offer limitation visible so the discount does not become misleading.

**WHAT average:** 7.90

**Changes for v2**
1. Add a mid-page estimate CTA after services.
2. Clarify callback destination language.
3. Keep “same-day service, call for details” precise.
4. Make image labels secondary, not essential for comprehension.

## HOW critique

### Massimo Vignelli — 8.1
- The grid is disciplined and the reduced palette fits the trade.
- The service card grid can be calmer if the borders stay but the copy gets shorter.
- The footer should be purely business-facing.

### Erik Spiekermann — 8.0
- Bebas Neue is right for contractor signage energy, but body copy needs enough size and contrast to hold up on mobile.
- The giant headings need slightly more tracking discipline and less vertical padding on smaller screens.
- Keep button language consistent.

### Dieter Rams — 8.2
- Remove any sentence that explains the redesign instead of the service.
- The page does not need extra proof widgets. The license, years, insurance, service area, and offer are enough.
- Good restraint on no stats bars and no fake reviews.

### Emil Kowalski — 8.1
- Motion should remain basic: reveal sections, stagger service cards, scale button active state.
- No image hover motion. The current photo board should stay static.
- Add visible default content for reduced-motion and no-JS conditions.

**HOW average:** 8.10

**Changes for v3**
1. Shorten service-card copy and reduce mobile section padding.
2. Normalize CTA labels.
3. Finalize restrained reveal motion only.
4. Remove all customer-facing source/provenance language.
