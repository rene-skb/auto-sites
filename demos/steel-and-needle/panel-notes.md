# Steel & Needle — Panel Notes

## WHY critique

**Steve Jobs — 8.0**
- The premium direction is right. The page finally treats the work like the headline instead of burying it in shop mechanics.
- Lead with the hybrid offer faster. The idea is not "tattoo studio with extras," it is a studio where piercing, tattooing, and jewelry already live together.

**Paul Graham — 7.6**
- The strongest move is honesty about what was and was not verified. Keep that.
- Some of the copy is still one layer too meta. Say the concrete thing first.

**David Ogilvy — 7.8**
- "Sharp work. Warm room. No posturing." is memorable, but the value proposition needs to arrive sooner.
- Use literal service words near the top. They sell.

**Panel average:** 7.8

**Next changes**
- Tighten the hero into a direct service-led promise
- Reduce meta explanation in the opening paragraph
- Keep the founder-energy proof, but put the real offer in front of it

## WHAT critique

**Don Norman — 7.8**
- The utility cards do useful work. Labels are clear and the page does not make people hunt for the basics.
- The CTA wording should stay literal, especially on a local-service page.

**Steve Krug — 8.0**
- I can get it quickly: where it is, what it does, where to click next.
- "See Instagram" is slightly softer than it needs to be. "View Instagram" or "Open Instagram" scans faster.

**Jakob Nielsen — 7.9**
- Good restraint on unknown facts. Better to omit than guess.
- Keep the proof section from looking like reviews if they are not reviews.

**Panel average:** 7.9

**Next changes**
- Make CTA labels even more literal
- Rename the proof section to avoid review-card expectations
- Keep scan-first card labels and visible next steps

## HOW critique

**Massimo Vignelli — 8.0**
- Strong hierarchy. The serif headline and restrained palette feel deliberate.
- The page works because it does not decorate too much.

**Erik Spiekermann — 8.2**
- Type choices are carrying the brand well. The sans stays readable and the display face has enough tension.
- Keep body copy tight. This subject gets muddy quickly when the paragraphs expand.

**Dieter Rams — 7.7**
- Good reduction. The page omits what cannot be verified.
- One more pass of wording cleanup helps remove the last traces of internal process language.

**Emil Kowalski — 8.1**
- Reveal motion is enough. No need for hover theatrics or image movement.
- Button press feedback is correct. Keep the rest quiet.

**Panel average:** 8.0

**Next changes**
- Keep motion to reveals and button feedback only
- Tighten any remaining internal-sounding phrasing
- Preserve the editorial rhythm without adding more chrome
