# Calgary Tattoo Company — panel notes

## WHY critique

### Steve Jobs — 7.7
- The good part is the page knows the sell is not mythology, it is confidence plus access.
- The first draft felt slightly too descriptive. The hero needed more bite.
- Keep the studio feeling dark and direct. Do not soften it with lifestyle fluff.

### Paul Graham — 7.4
- The facts are strong. Lead with them faster.
- The useful difference here is range plus walk-in practicality. Say that plainly.
- A few lines still sounded like website copy instead of a person helping you choose a shop.

### David Ogilvy — 8.0
- “Walk in for black and grey, portraits, colour realism, and traditional tattooing” is specific enough to earn attention.
- The artist section is the real sales section. Make it easier to scan.
- The FAQ-derived proof is persuasive because it contains numbers and policy, not adjectives.

**Panel average:** 7.7

**Next changes**
1. Sharpen the hero into a cleaner reason to visit now.
2. Make artist cards and proof blocks scan in fewer seconds.
3. Cut any leftover studio filler language.

## WHAT critique

### Don Norman — 7.8
- The structure works because it moves from choice to evidence to logistics.
- The path to contact is clear, but the visit section should feel even more literal.
- Good decision keeping one main CTA language throughout.

### Steve Krug — 7.6
- I know what this place is in one screen. Good.
- The artist section did too much sentence work at first. Tightening it helps.
- The page stays readable because there are no gimmick cards fighting each other.

### Jakob Nielsen — 7.4
- Practical trust signals are strong: hours, pricing, deposits, aftercare.
- The map plus address plus phone block is enough. Avoid repeating the same utility copy elsewhere.
- Contrast and touch targets are fine on mobile-sized components.

**Panel average:** 7.6

**Next changes**
1. Tighten section intros and remove repeated logistics copy.
2. Keep CTA labels literal and consistent.
3. Preserve clean hierarchy in the visit block.

## HOW critique

### Massimo Vignelli — 7.9
- The type system is disciplined enough. The serif earns the atmosphere.
- Better once the spacing opens and the palette stays reduced.
- Let the images breathe. The work is the ornament.

### Erik Spiekermann — 7.8
- Body text is readable because the sans stays plain and the serif does not leak into utility copy.
- The hierarchy improved once the card intros shortened.
- Keep small text bright enough against the dark panels.

### Dieter Rams — 7.6
- The useful parts are good. Anything decorative beyond that would hurt it.
- The proof section earns itself because it answers real pre-booking questions.
- Fewer visual treatments, more consistency. The current restraint is the right direction.

### Emil Kowalski — 8.1
- Motion should stay invisible: reveal on scroll, stagger in lists, button press feedback.
- Good call avoiding image hover effects.
- The hero image already has enough drama. Do not animate it aggressively.

**Panel average:** 7.85

**Next changes**
1. Keep motion to reveal and response only.
2. Maintain one coherent dark editorial system.
3. Protect the image cadence and do not add extra photo sections.
