Our Story

From Chiapas to Douglas Street

When Maritza Sanchez moved to Victoria 37 years ago, there were maybe four Latin American families in the entire city. Finding the ingredients to cook the food she grew up with meant a trip to Vancouver or Toronto.

In 2011, Maritza and her family opened Mexican House of Spice on Douglas Street. Her mother — who's from the southern Mexican province of Chiapas — still works in the store.

Today, families drive in from across Vancouver Island, some coming all the way from Tofino, just to stock up on the foods they can't find anywhere else.

"Immigrants: that's our focus. We try to do the lower market price so people can afford a little bit more." Maritza Sanchez, Owner

What We Carry

More Than Just Mexico

01

Spices & Dried Chillies

The best selection of dried chillies in the city. Ancho, guajillo, chipotle, pasilla — plus cumin, Mexican oregano, and seasonings you won't find at the supermarket.

02

Fresh & Frozen Staples

Masa, fresh tortillas, queso fresco, panela cheese, frozen tamales, and plantains. The building blocks of home cooking from Mexico to Colombia.

03

Sauces, Snacks & Drinks

Walls of hot sauce. De la Rosa peanut chocolates. Imported soft drinks from Colombia to Argentina. Spicy snacks from every corner of Latin America.

04

Beyond Mexico

African and Jamaican ingredients. Community-baked conchas. Seasonal specialties like King's bread for Epiphany. Piñatas and Indigenous art — this is a cultural hub.

The Name

It Says Mexico.
It Means Everywhere.

The store is named for Maritza's mother, who's from Chiapas, Mexico. But the shelves tell a bigger story — Colombia, Guatemala, Argentina, Jamaica, Nigeria. Every shelf is someone's home.

Fresh baked goods come from immigrant bakers in the community. The piñatas overhead are handmade by local families. The Indigenous art in the back is displayed for sale. This isn't a grocery store. It's what happens when a family decides their city needs something it doesn't have yet.
