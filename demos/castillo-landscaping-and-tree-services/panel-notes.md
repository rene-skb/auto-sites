# Panel notes — Castillo Landscaping and Tree Services

## WHY critique

**Steve Jobs — 8.1**
- The earthy editorial system has confidence. It feels far more credible than the current site.
- The first version leaned too polished with a garden hero. The real lift/tree-work shot tells the truth faster.
- Make the promise immediate: licensed LA tree crew, free estimate, phone-first.

**Paul Graham — 7.8**
- The headline is clean, but v0 had a few sentences that sounded like a redesign audit.
- Cut “current site lists” and anything that explains the source instead of speaking to the customer.
- Keep the family history in the proof/story section, not the hero.

**David Ogilvy — 8.0**
- “Licensed tree crews for LA yards” is more specific than “tree work without the runaround.”
- The phone number and 24-hour availability are the ad. Keep them visible.
- Remove any image or line that creates doubt, especially the old poster with a mismatched phone number.

**Panel average:** 7.97

**Next changes**
1. Swap hero to real tree-service imagery.
2. Make LA/Pacoima/license/free-estimate specificity show above the fold.
3. Remove internal source-language from customer copy.
4. Remove the conflicting-phone-number poster image.

## WHAT critique

**Don Norman — 8.1**
- The page explains the business quickly: tree crew, LA/Pacoima, license, phone, free estimate.
- “Open 24 hours” needs precision. It may mean phone/form intake, not guaranteed emergency dispatch.
- Business naming should be consistent so the customer does not wonder if this is a different company.

**Steve Krug — 8.0**
- The call path is obvious, which matters most here.
- “Proof” in the nav is vague. “License & Reviews” tells me what I’ll get.
- Long email addresses need wrapping, and mobile needs a persistent call affordance.

**Jakob Nielsen — 7.9**
- Service categories scan well, but tree work should remain the primary path.
- The repeated phone number is useful, not redundant, for local services.
- Clarify 24-hour wording and avoid implying unsupported emergency response.

**Panel average:** 8.00

**Next changes**
1. Normalize the business name across header, metadata, visible copy, and footer.
2. Rename proof navigation for recognition.
3. Clarify 24-hour call language.
4. Add a mobile sticky call CTA and robust long-email wrapping.

## HOW critique

**Massimo Vignelli — 8.1**
- The grid is sound and the palette belongs to the subject.
- The contact strip is too tabular for the rest of the editorial system.
- Reduce the repeated slab scale so the hero owns the typographic peak.

**Erik Spiekermann — 8.1**
- The slab face has good trade confidence, but it gets loud when every section uses it at maximum size.
- Nav labels are small and tight. Keep them functional, not decorative.
- The service cards need consistent rhythm more than more ornament.

**Dieter Rams — 8.0**
- Many cards, many rounded corners. The system is consistent, but over-applied.
- Remove visual weight from the quick facts. They are utility, not a feature.
- Cut repeated proof where possible and let the phone path stay dominant.

**Emil Kowalski — 8.2**
- Motion should be quiet: section reveals, staggered cards, button active states.
- No parallax, no image zoom, no bouncy contractor theatrics.
- Mobile sticky call is appropriate because it supports the primary task.

**Panel average:** 8.10

**Next changes**
1. Soften quick facts and reduce table-like dividers.
2. Tighten vertical rhythm and oversized repeated headlines.
3. Reduce rounded-card repetition slightly.
4. Keep motion limited to reveals, button feedback, and sticky mobile call utility.
