# Panel notes — Bobcat Masonry and Concrete

## WHY critique

### Steve Jobs — 7.9
- The contractor-board direction fits Bobcat better than a polished luxury construction page.
- The hero is specific, but the headline reads like a service list. It needs a little more conviction around property work.
- The photo collage helps because the job proof is real, not decorative.

### Paul Graham — 7.7
- Clearer than the current site. A homeowner can tell what Bobcat does in a few seconds.
- Cut any copy that sounds like an internal audit, especially around sources and archives.
- The page should say “what to call about” faster, then let the service chips handle breadth.

### David Ogilvy — 8.0
- The headline is concrete, literally and verbally. Good.
- The strongest promise is “free estimate for the actual hardscape problem.” Bring that into the first fold.
- Use the license and hours as reassurance, not as the main sell.

**WHY average:** 7.87

**Changes for v1**
1. Give the hero a stronger contractor-specific promise instead of a pure noun list.
2. Move “free estimate” closer to the first-fold value proposition.
3. Strip any visible archive/source-ish phrasing from customer-facing copy.
4. Keep the real project collage and dark worksite tone.

## WHAT critique

### Don Norman — 8.0
- The page answers what Bobcat does, where they work, and how to call.
- The service taxonomy is broad but understandable.
- Add a clearer split between “concrete” and “masonry” so visitors do not have to parse every service chip.

### Steve Krug — 7.8
- The contact path is obvious. The sticky mobile call is good.
- The “Real project photos beat contractor clip art” headline is cute but talks about the demo, not the customer’s job.
- The project section should label project types before city names, because users scan for work like theirs.

### Jakob Nielsen — 7.8
- Quick facts are useful and concise.
- The services chip list is long. Keep it, but make it subordinate to the four main buckets.
- Avoid “5 counties” as a proof headline if the service-area list is already explicit.

**WHAT average:** 7.87

**Changes for v2**
1. Replace the project-section meta headline with customer-facing proof language.
2. Add concrete/masonry split cues in the services section.
3. Replace “5 counties” proof headline with “Service area” to avoid stats-card energy.
4. Keep contact CTAs direct and visible.

## HOW critique

### Massimo Vignelli — 8.1
- The grid has authority. Dark-to-cream-to-dark pacing works.
- The hero collage rotation is acceptable but should be quieter, especially on desktop.
- Typography is strong, but the service-section headline runs long.

### Erik Spiekermann — 8.0
- Archivo Black + Public Sans fits the job-site voice.
- Some labels are too letter-spaced on small captions.
- Body copy contrast is good on dark sections, but the cream section can use slightly tighter measures.

### Dieter Rams — 8.2
- No fake stats bar, no fake reviews, no image hover effects. Good discipline.
- The service chip cloud is useful but close to clutter. Reduce chip count or make it quieter.
- The final estimate panel earns its space.

### Emil Kowalski — 8.1
- Scroll reveal and button active states are present.
- Shorten reveal delay on long pages so content does not feel staged.
- Keep motion to opacity and translateY only. No image motion needed.

**HOW average:** 8.10

**Changes for v3**
1. Quiet the hero collage rotation and reduce heavy visual tilt.
2. Tighten the services headline and chip styling.
3. Reduce reveal delay ceiling from 220ms to 180ms.
4. Preserve no-hover-image rule and reduced-motion support.
