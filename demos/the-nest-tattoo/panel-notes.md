# The Nest Tattoo — Panel Notes

## Phase 4 — WHY Panel on v0

### Steve Jobs — 7.0
- The page looks premium and calm, but the verbal idea is softer than the visual idea.
- "A quieter place to get tattooed" sounds elegant, but it is mood-first instead of advantage-first.
- The page needs a sharper reason this specific studio wins beyond cleaner and calmer.

### Paul Graham — 6.5
- The main thesis is still fuzzy. The page tries to sell atmosphere, artist quality, walk-ins, and location all at once.
- The hero says something nice, not something decisive.
- Specific details are present, but the page still risks feeling like a good tattoo template instead of a page built from one sharp insight.

### David Ogilvy — 7.5
- The page is organized and readable, but the headline does not carry a memorable selling proposition.
- Too much copy leans on safe language like calm, friendly, and personal.
- The next version should replace reassurance with proof: artist matching, walk-in logic, Old Town convenience, and tighter portfolio-led selling.

### WHY panel average
- **7.0 / 10**

### Next changes for v1
1. Rewrite the hero around a harder benefit, not just mood.
2. Pick one main thesis: artist-led Old Town tattoos without the usual shop-floor chaos.
3. Tighten generic reassurance into concrete proof.
4. Make the above-the-fold message answer what it is, why to choose it, and what to do next.

## Phase 6 — WHAT Panel on v1

### Don Norman — 7.4
- The page has strong taste, but the top of the page still makes users work a little too hard to extract the practical decision path.
- The artist split is useful, though the utility details should arrive even faster after the hero.
- Some copy blocks are still longer than they need to be for a walk-in local business.

### Steve Krug — 7.7
- Better. The headline is clearer and the page reads faster now.
- Still, the first screen could make the next action more obvious. A user should know where to click, where it is, and whether they can just go.
- The page should reduce a bit of conceptual explanation and let utility and portfolio do more of the work.

### Jakob Nielsen — 7.5
- Good hierarchy overall, but information scent can improve. Address, hours, and walk-in status are key recognition cues and should stay closer to the top.
- External actions are visible, but the primary CTA cluster can be simplified.
- The page is visually consistent, though the proof section could do more functional work.

### WHAT panel average
- **7.53 / 10**

### Next changes for v2
1. Add a compact utility strip directly under the hero with address, hours, and walk-in status.
2. Shorten explanatory copy in the proof area and let the artist cards carry more of the page.
3. Simplify the CTA set so one primary action leads and the secondary action supports it.
4. Make the page feel even more usable at a glance, especially for someone deciding quickly on mobile.

## Phase 8 — HOW Panel on v2

### Massimo Vignelli — 7.7
- The system is coherent, but a few sections still feel like separate good ideas rather than one disciplined page language.
- Card treatments are consistent, though the utility strip plus later detail cards create a little repetition.
- Typography is strong, but the lower page can be reduced so the hero and artist sections stay dominant.

### Erik Spiekermann — 7.8
- The type pairing works. Fraunces gives the right editorial mood and Manrope keeps the utility clear.
- Hierarchy is mostly good, but some smaller cards compete for attention because they share similar weight and spacing.
- Tightening the later sections would make the reading path cleaner.

### Dieter Rams — 7.5
- Good restraint overall. The page feels designed, not decorated.
- Some explanatory blocks can still be simplified. The practical details repeat themselves once the utility strip already did the job.
- Less, but slightly better organized, would improve this.

### Emil Kowalski — 8.0
- Motion foundations are already solid: reveal rhythm is restrained, active states are present, and the page feels ready for polish.
- The main improvement is structural. Cleaner section transitions would make motion feel more intentional.
- The hero and gallery are the strongest visual moments. Let them breathe a little more.

### HOW panel average
- **7.75 / 10**

### Next changes for v3
1. Reduce duplication between the utility strip, proof, and detail sections.
2. Keep the artist and gallery moments dominant, with a cleaner glide into the visit section.
3. Tighten copy in the lower half and keep card hierarchy from flattening out.
4. Preserve the restrained motion system and avoid adding decorative animation.
