# Panel notes — Universal Landscaping

## WHY critique

**Steve Jobs — 7.8**
- The cleanup idea lands because it is specific to the lived customer fear: the crew makes a bigger mess than the yard.
- The first pass felt slightly too magazine-like for a Pasadena yard crew. Keep the confidence, but let the phone and estimate path carry more weight.
- The real project images and truck make the page feel more grounded than generated lawn glamour.

**Paul Graham — 8.0**
- Clear enough: lawn care, sprinkler repair, cleanup, tree work, Pasadena.
- Remove any copy that explains the redesign or the source material. Customers do not care how the page was made.
- Repeat the call action. This business closes through conversation, not browsing.

**David Ogilvy — 7.6**
- “A yard crew that cleans up after itself” is memorable and review-backed.
- The subhead needs direct service terms so search intent and human intent both get served.
- Reviews should stay attributed with first name + last initial, dates/source when available.

**WHY average:** 7.8

**Changes for v1**
1. Replace internal/source copy with customer-facing language.
2. Tighten vertical rhythm.
3. Add another phone-first CTA after services.
4. Keep the cleanup headline, but make the subhead more direct.

## WHAT critique

**Don Norman — 8.0**
- The service categories are understandable and organized around real homeowner needs.
- The phone CTA is visible, but the page is long enough to need repeated action points.
- Contact details should include one large call button before the info rows.

**Steve Krug — 8.1**
- Visitors can tell what the business does without thinking.
- “Tree + hedge work” should become “Tree trimming + hedge work” for faster comprehension.
- The work section had one meta sentence that sounded like strategy notes. Replace it.

**Jakob Nielsen — 7.8**
- Scannability is good, especially the four service cards and review attributions.
- Large serif section heads slow scanning when every section is equally dramatic.
- Footer “demo redesign” language should not be the lead credibility note.

**WHAT average:** 7.97

**Changes for v2**
1. Make the hero subhead list the concrete services.
2. Add CTAs after reviews and project range.
3. Reduce lower-section headline size.
4. Add a large call button inside the contact card.

## HOW critique

**Massimo Vignelli — 8.0**
- The system has discipline: green, cream, clay, strong grid, no extra colors.
- The type is a little theatrical for the category. Keep the contrast but loosen the hero and reduce lower-section dominance.
- The image collage should serve trust, not decoration.

**Erik Spiekermann — 8.2**
- Fraunces + DM Sans gives the business a recognizable voice.
- Tight display leading risks making the headline feel cramped. Loosen it slightly.
- Body copy should be darker; muted green text is close to underconfident in long paragraphs.

**Dieter Rams — 8.0**
- The page has too many decorative proof assets. Remove anything low-res or ornamental.
- The logo at the bottom is not strong enough to feature large.
- The best details are useful: phone, service area, real review names, clear services.

**Emil Kowalski — 8.1**
- Motion should stay dependable: scroll reveal, small button feedback, restrained hover on service cards only.
- No image zoom on static photos.
- Respect reduced motion and keep transitions property-specific.

**HOW average:** 8.08

**Changes for v3/v4**
1. Loosen hero type and reduce section padding.
2. Darken body copy.
3. Replace low-res logo feature with a service-area graphic.
4. Keep motion to scroll reveal, button active state, and subtle service-card lift.

## Lucy self-review

**Self-review score:** 8.3

The final page is materially stronger than the source site: it has a direct service promise, clearer service categories, real review proof, bilingual/local trust signals, OG tags, motion with reduced-motion support, and a phone-first conversion path. The weakest point is still asset quality. The real project images are useful but inconsistent; generated support images are polished but cannot fully replace a proper before/after gallery.
