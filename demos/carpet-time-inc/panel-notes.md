# Panel Notes - Carpet Time Inc

## Creative direction
Carpet Time should not feel like a generic flooring catalog. The useful idea is decision support: showroom selection, accurate measurement, and an experienced installation crew. The visual system should feel warm, material, and residential without pretending to show actual customer homes.

## WHY critique

### Steve Jobs - 8.0
- The page has a clear point of view: choosing flooring is the job, not browsing an endless product menu.
- The hero needs to make the comfort/tactility of carpet more emotional without becoming sentimental.
- Keep the family-owned proof, but make it serve confidence rather than nostalgia.

### Paul Graham - 7.7
- The strongest line is the floor choosing you: it names the user's anxiety simply.
- Cut anything that sounds like it is commenting on the old website. The visitor should not see the audit.
- The material categories are clear, but the page should explain why someone calls before choosing.

### David Ogilvy - 8.1
- "Pick the floor before the floor picks you" has a useful hook and carries a concrete problem.
- The CSLB number and long-tenured installer proof are persuasive. Put them where people can scan them.
- Avoid abstract words like quality unless paired with a specific proof point.

**WHY average:** 7.93

**Changes for v1:**
1. Push estimate/readiness language higher.
2. Tie family-owned since 1989 to judgment, not heritage fluff.
3. Remove visible audit/source framing from customer copy.
4. Put factual proof in a stronger chapter.

## WHAT critique

### Don Norman - 8.2
- The user path is understandable: choose material, measure space, install with a known crew.
- The estimate checklist reduces uncertainty and should stay.
- Make hours/contact unavoidable without repeating them too many times.

### Steve Krug - 8.0
- Five-second read: Riverside flooring, free estimate, many materials. Good.
- Material cards work because they mirror how visitors self-identify.
- The top navigation is short enough. Keep it that way.

### Jakob Nielsen - 7.8
- Body sizes and contrast are mostly safe.
- Confirm mobile card stacking and tap target sizes.
- Do not rely on the image content alone for material categories; labels need to stay explicit.

**WHAT average:** 8.00

**Changes for v2:**
1. Add/retain compact quick-info strip under hero.
2. Make material labels literal and visible.
3. Keep estimate CTA labels action-specific.
4. Preserve Google Maps business-listing link, not generic address link.

## HOW critique

### Massimo Vignelli - 8.1
- The chapter structure is coherent: warm hero, dark material room, proof block, estimate desk, dark visit close.
- The grid is strongest when it uses fewer, larger modules.
- Avoid adding another photo section; the official imagery has done enough.

### Erik Spiekermann - 8.0
- Fraunces and Manrope are appropriate: soft editorial display plus hard-working interface sans.
- Keep display type large but body text plain and readable.
- Do not use tiny uppercase labels below 15px equivalent.

### Dieter Rams - 8.2
- The site is better when it behaves like a guide, not a brochure.
- The visualizer image is useful; the generic contact image is not.
- No stats bar. The proof cards carry enough evidence.

### Emil Kowalski - 8.1
- Motion should be basic reveal only. No image hover or material-card spectacle.
- Button active states are enough for interaction feedback.
- Respect reduced motion and keep all content visible without JavaScript.

**HOW average:** 8.10

**Changes for v3/v4:**
1. Keep two major photo moments and skip generic support imagery.
2. Add restrained scroll reveals and button active state.
3. Tighten dark-section contrast.
4. Keep card treatments consistent.

## Final self-review
- **Score:** 8.5
- The page is specific to Carpet Time's real strengths: family-owned since 1989, CSLB #630569, material breadth, free in-home measurements, commercial/residential scope, and Riverside showroom logistics.
- It avoids fake customer reviews, invented prices, fabricated warranties, and generic map/address links.
- Weak spot: no true project gallery or attributed customer reviews were verified, so the design leans on official manufacturer-style imagery and factual proof. That caps the emotional ceiling below a real photo-rich contractor/gallery build.
