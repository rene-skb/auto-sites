# Panel notes — Ed’s Landscaping

## WHY critique

### Steve Jobs — 8.0
- The page has a real thesis: Ed’s builds yards people can use, not just look at.
- The first hero photo feels too polished and residential-listing-like. Lead with an image that feels more like outdoor work and less like a house ad.
- The trust badges work, but they need to feel like quick proof, not a stats strip.

### Paul Graham — 7.8
- The copy is mostly specific and grounded, but a few lines explain the source logic instead of talking to the customer.
- The review section says the useful thing: show-up-and-follow-through. Keep that. Make it less visually inflated.
- The service area sentence is too dense. Break it apart.

### David Ogilvy — 8.1
- “Build the yard you’ll actually use” is clear and sellable.
- The 45+ years and license should stay near the top because they answer the trust question fast.
- The contact section should sell the phone call more directly. People with yard problems want the next step.

**WHY average:** 7.97

**Changes for v1**
1. Swap the hero image to a more landscape-specific project photo.
2. Reduce mid-page headline scale and review card height.
3. Break the service-area copy into scan-friendly chips.
4. Tighten source/meta-feeling copy in services and gallery.

## WHAT critique

### Don Norman — 8.0
- The overall flow is understandable: value, services, proof, process, contact.
- Contact comes too late for a service business. A compact call strip after the hero would reduce effort.
- The footer exposes demo/source language and breaks the illusion.

### Steve Krug — 8.1
- The page scans well, but the service cards ask people to read too much.
- The primary action should stay boring and obvious: call for a free estimate.
- Repeated images make the page feel thinner than it is.

### Jakob Nielsen — 7.8
- Navigation labels are clear and the phone link is easy to find.
- The service area list works better as chips than a paragraph.
- Mobile needs an always-available call action because the main contact section is far down the page.

**WHAT average:** 7.97

**Changes for v2**
1. Remove visible demo/source footer language.
2. Add a compact phone-first contact strip after the hero.
3. Shorten service-card copy into scannable service fragments.
4. Remove duplicate image use across final visible sections.

## HOW critique

### Massimo Vignelli — 8.0
- The page has a coherent grid and a restrained material palette.
- Section spacing is a little generous for a phone-first service business. Tighten the rhythm.
- The trust badges should stay quiet and structural, not decorative.

### Erik Spiekermann — 8.2
- Libre Baskerville gives the old-contractor trust layer without feeling antique.
- Some body copy needs slightly more line-height, especially review and process text.
- Navigation can gain a little size for legibility.

### Dieter Rams — 7.9
- The page can lose a small amount of vertical padding and become more useful.
- The estimate card has too much empty space compared with the information it carries.
- No extra image motion. The photos should stay still.

### Emil Kowalski — 8.1
- Keep the existing reveal system. It is enough.
- Stagger hero content and cards, but respect reduced motion.
- Button active feedback is in place. Do not animate static photos.

**HOW average:** 8.05

**Changes for v3**
1. Tighten global section spacing and the services handoff.
2. Improve body/review line-height and nav legibility.
3. Lighten the hero overlay and soften trust-badge dividers.
4. Reduce the contact card’s perceived empty space.
