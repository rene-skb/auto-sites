# Panel notes — Lord Tile

## Creative direction

Lord Tile should feel like an old-school licensed tile contractor with enough polish for Pasadena bathroom and kitchen remodel work. The pitch is trust first: Dave, C-54 license #983081, established 1997, 30+ years, tile repair through full bathroom remodels.

Hero pattern selected: split-image-right. Recent builds used editorial-spread, image-top-text-below, and other service layouts. This build uses a tighter contractor-trust split with a large bath image and early quote actions.

## WHY critique

### Steve Jobs — 8.0
- The v0 direction lands because it turns “old school” into a strength instead of hiding it under generic luxury language.
- The hero needs a sharper first sentence. “Tile work that still looks right years later” is the real idea.
- Keep Dave visible. The personal contractor proof is stronger than another service grid.

### Paul Graham — 7.8
- Clear and specific. The page names tile, Pasadena, license, hours, phone, and actual services.
- Cut contractor-fluff words like “premium” and “dream space” where they slipped in.
- The order should be: trust, work examples, services, reviews, contact.

### David Ogilvy — 8.2
- The strongest copy asset is “Old School Tile Contractor.” Use it, but pair it with a benefit.
- The consultation CTA is practical. Mention call and email above the fold.
- Reviews work because they mention cracked shower tiles, backsplash, bathrooms, and honest advice.

**WHY average:** 8.0

**Next changes**
1. Rewrite hero around durability and old-school tile work.
2. Move license and established-date proof closer to the headline.
3. Trim abstract copy and keep every paragraph under 3 sentences.
4. Make Dave's consultation CTA more direct.

## WHAT critique

### Don Norman — 8.1
- v1 explains what visitors can do next: call, email, review services, and request consultation.
- The service cards are scannable, but shower repair and bathroom remodel should feel more prominent because reviews support those use cases.
- Contact information is easy to find, which matters more than decorative storytelling here.

### Steve Krug — 8.0
- I know what this is in 5 seconds: Pasadena tile contractor, licensed, call for quote.
- The page has enough labels and action buttons, but “See the work” should anchor to the gallery.
- Avoid making visitors parse too many service names at once.

### Jakob Nielsen — 7.8
- Strong early information scent: phone, license, hours, Pasadena.
- Mobile needs short cards and bigger tap targets.
- Keep the footer contact block plain. A contractor page should not bury the phone number.

**WHAT average:** 7.97

**Next changes**
1. Add anchor links that match section labels.
2. Promote bathroom/shower and backsplash/project repair services in grouped cards.
3. Tighten mobile spacing and CTA tap targets.
4. Repeat phone/email in final contact section without visual clutter.

## HOW critique

### Massimo Vignelli — 8.1
- The restrained stone palette works. It feels like tile, grout, and warm Pasadena light.
- The grid is cleaner when the gallery uses equal-height square-ish images instead of a fussy masonry attempt.
- Reduce decorative borders. Let spacing do the work.

### Erik Spiekermann — 8.0
- Newsreader gives the “old school” phrase authority without looking antique.
- Instrument Sans is practical for services and contact details.
- Watch the uppercase labels. Too much letter spacing can feel contractor-brochure stiff.

### Dieter Rams — 8.0
- Useful, understandable, honest. Good.
- Remove the extra “material types” section if it repeats services.
- Motion should be quiet. This is a contractor, not a fashion site.

### Emil Kowalski — 8.3
- Add standard reveal motion only: hero lines, cards, gallery, review cards.
- Use opacity and translateY, no image hover zoom.
- Button active states need the 0.97 scale and custom easing.

**HOW average:** 8.1

**Next changes**
1. Use equal gallery cards and remove redundant material clutter.
2. Tune labels and spacing so the page feels calmer.
3. Add scroll reveal with reduced-motion support.
4. Keep images static except reveal on scroll.
