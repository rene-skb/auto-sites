# Panel Notes - Joseph & Sons Tile

## WHY critique

### Steve Jobs - 8.1
- The central idea lands: tile work must endure ordinary life, not just photograph well.
- The dark immersive opening feels more distinctive than a typical contractor page.
- The hero is a touch too loud; the image gets buried under the overlay.

### Paul Graham - 7.9
- The page says real things: 60+ years, free consultation, suppliers, exact services.
- Cut or compress repetition around “official site” language so the page feels customer-facing, not like an audit.
- The service value is clear, but the first scroll could tighten faster.

### David Ogilvy - 8.2
- “Tile work that has to live with you” is specific and memorable enough to carry the page.
- The call CTA is direct and practical.
- Make the proof line less defensive. “No invented testimonials here” is honest, but visible copy should sell the business, not explain sourcing.

**WHY average:** 8.07

**Next changes**
1. Reduce hero size slightly and let the image breathe.
2. Improve nav/link contrast.
3. Replace source-discipline visible wording with customer-facing proof language.
4. Tighten vertical rhythm after the consultation section.

## WHAT critique

### Don Norman - 8.0
- The visitor understands “tile contractor” eventually, but the hero should say tile installation and service area faster.
- The practical path is present but not explicit enough: call, describe project, discuss material/labor, then schedule.
- Captions would turn the image gallery from decoration into proof.

### Steve Krug - 7.8
- Remove visible “source site / official site” language. People should not have to parse why the copy is sourced.
- Add a compact services list near the top so visitors can self-select in seconds.
- The headings are elegant but a few can be more literal.

### Jakob Nielsen - 8.0
- Good CTA repetition and phone visibility.
- Service area and availability need plain-language treatment.
- Do not add an unsupported license/insurance claim; the absence is better than inventing proof.

**WHAT average:** 7.93

**Next changes**
1. Make hero headline more direct: tile installation in LA + Orange County.
2. Add above-fold service chips.
3. Remove “official/source site” phrasing from customer-facing sections.
4. Add gallery captions and a clear estimate flow.

## HOW critique

### Massimo Vignelli - 8.1
- The system is coherent, but the serif scale is doing too much work in too many places.
- Tighten section rhythm and reduce decorative borders so the grid feels more inevitable.
- Keep the three-surface palette: cream, sand, charcoal.

### Erik Spiekermann - 8.0
- Fraunces gives the tile work tactility, but card headings should feel more utilitarian.
- Navigation, facts, chips, and CTAs need stronger sans discipline.
- Shorter contractor language will make the type feel more confident.

### Dieter Rams - 8.0
- The useful parts are there; remove the meta phrases and repeated CTA padding.
- Reduce the hero chips and make the proof section direct.
- The page should feel easier to use, not merely more designed.

### Emil Kowalski - 8.2
- Use the existing scroll reveal pattern, stagger cards, and keep button active states.
- No parallax and no image hover zoom. Motion should make the page feel loaded and calm.
- Reduce transitions to transform/opacity and respect reduced motion.

**HOW average:** 8.08

**Next changes**
1. Reduce global section and headline scale.
2. Simplify hero chips to four core services.
3. Replace “proof without padding” with direct contractor proof.
4. Keep restrained reveal motion; no decorative hover on images.
