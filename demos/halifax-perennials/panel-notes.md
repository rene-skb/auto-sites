# Halifax Perennials — Panel Notes

## Phase 4 — WHY critique on v0

### Steve Jobs — 7.8
- The inventory breadth is there, but the first version needed a cleaner promise than "plant nursery, but nicer."
- The reserve-now and pick-up-later mechanic is the real product. Lead with that.
- The page feels better when it acts like a practical seasonal storefront, not a mood board.

### Paul Graham — 7.5
- The strongest sentences are the literal ones: reserve now, pickup later, hours change week to week.
- Cut any sentence that explains the redesign too much. The page should prove the point by being clearer.
- Keep the copy grounded in what the business actually does.

### David Ogilvy — 8.0
- "Reserve spring plants before pickup season gets crowded" is sharper because it carries urgency without sounding fake.
- The best selling points are specific: roses, peonies, fruiting plants, no Saturday pickups, Facebook hours.
- The business already has plenty to say. The page just needs to arrange it.

### WHY panel average
- **7.77 / 10**

### Next changes for v1
1. Sharpen the hero around reserve-now timing.
2. Reduce rescue-process language in the hero.
3. Keep practical details visible above the fold.
4. Let the business model do the persuasion.

## Phase 6 — WHAT critique on v1

### Don Norman — 7.7
- The page gets the idea across, but users need the contact and pickup facts even faster.
- A dedicated quick-info strip helps visitors who are already close to ordering.
- The pickup system is the page's main interaction model. Surface it early.

### Steve Krug — 8.0
- Better. I can tell what this place is and what to do next without thinking too hard.
- Still, practical details should hit in one fast pass: address, phone, current hours source, and catalogue action.
- The page works best when it behaves like a storefront, not an essay.

### Jakob Nielsen — 7.6
- Recognition cues are solid once the quick details are visible.
- External actions are clear, but copy below the fold should stay lean so the hierarchy remains obvious.
- The page benefits from repeating less and grouping operational details tightly.

### WHAT panel average
- **7.77 / 10**

### Next changes for v2
1. Add a fast utility strip under the hero.
2. Trim explanatory copy in proof and seasonal sections.
3. Keep one obvious primary action with supporting actions beneath it.
4. Preserve scannability on mobile.

## Phase 8 — HOW critique on v2

### Massimo Vignelli — 7.8
- The system is coherent and the page has one visual language instead of several unrelated ones.
- The strongest move is the restrained palette paired with editorial serif display type.
- Tightening repetition between proof and visit content makes the whole page feel more resolved.

### Erik Spiekermann — 7.9
- Cormorant Garamond gives this the right horticultural elegance, and Instrument Sans keeps the logistics readable.
- Hierarchy is doing its job. The quick strip, hero, and visit block each have clear roles.
- Keep the card language consistent and avoid adding extra decorative flourishes.

### Dieter Rams — 7.7
- The page is better once it stops repeating the same operational point in three places.
- The gallery and quick strip earn their space. Anything beyond that would start to feel decorative.
- Less, but cleaner, is the right move here.

### Emil Kowalski — 8.1
- Standard reveal motion fits the page. Nothing needs spectacle.
- The button feedback and section reveals are enough to keep it feeling alive.
- The page already has strong natural pauses, so motion should simply support those transitions.

### HOW panel average
- **7.88 / 10**

### Next changes for v3
1. Reduce repeated operational copy.
2. Keep the photo moments and quick strip dominant.
3. Use restrained reveal motion only.
4. Preserve a calm reading rhythm from hero to visit section.
