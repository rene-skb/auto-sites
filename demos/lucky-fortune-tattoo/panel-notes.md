# Lucky Fortune Tattoo — Panel Notes

## WHY critique
**Reviewers:** Steve Jobs, Paul Graham, David Ogilvy

- **Steve Jobs — 7.6**
  - The dead-site-to-live-shop contrast is a real idea, but the first screen still needs a little more inevitability.
  - The image is strong enough to carry the page. Let the headline stay street-level and direct.
  - Move one practical proof point closer to the hero so the promise lands faster.
- **Paul Graham — 7.4**
  - The copy is mostly clear. Good instinct not to mythologize the shop.
  - Tighten any sentence that explains the strategy instead of just showing the facts.
  - Keep the page focused on what a person in Victoria needs right now.
- **David Ogilvy — 7.9**
  - “Walk-ins welcome on Pandora” is specific and serviceable.
  - The next lines should sharpen the phone-first behavior and the kind of work the shop is known for.
  - The proof cards should sound less like analysis and more like reasons to trust the shop.

**Panel average:** 7.63

**Next changes for Lucy**
1. Tighten the hero subcopy so it sounds more like the shop and less like commentary.
2. Pull one practical fact into stronger visual priority right below the headline.
3. Rewrite proof cards for cleaner, more literal language.

## WHAT critique
**Reviewers:** Don Norman, Steve Krug, Jakob Nielsen

- **Don Norman — 7.7**
  - The page makes the shop model understandable fast.
  - Utility is strongest when the address, hours, and phone read as one system.
  - Clarify why the map action exists and keep it adjacent to visit info.
- **Steve Krug — 8.0**
  - Easy to scan. I know where to look and what to click.
  - The “How the page should frame the shop” section is useful, but one sentence there can be shorter.
  - Good choice to avoid a fake review grid without verified names.
- **Jakob Nielsen — 7.3**
  - Contrast is good enough, but a couple supporting text blocks can be slightly brighter.
  - Keep CTA labels literal. The phone button is strong.
  - On mobile, stacked utility cards are the right move.

**Panel average:** 7.67

**Next changes for Lucy**
1. Brighten a few muted text blocks for stronger readability.
2. Shorten the explanatory story copy by one sentence.
3. Make the utility note and map action feel more directly tied to visit intent.

## HOW critique
**Reviewers:** Massimo Vignelli, Erik Spiekermann, Dieter Rams, Emil Kowalski

- **Massimo Vignelli — 7.5**
  - The structure is coherent. The big image and the dark utility shell are doing real work.
  - Keep the spacing disciplined. Let the sections breathe without adding ornament.
- **Erik Spiekermann — 7.8**
  - Cinzel gives the shop the right amount of ceremony.
  - Inter is doing the practical lifting well, but some support copy should be a touch firmer in color.
- **Dieter Rams — 7.4**
  - The page is restrained enough.
  - The strongest move is saying less and letting the work and utility carry the case.
  - Keep the motion invisible unless it improves reading order.
- **Emil Kowalski — 8.0**
  - Motion should stay in reveal timing and button response only.
  - No hover tricks on images, good.
  - Use stagger lightly so the page feels polished, not theatrical.

**Panel average:** 7.68

**Next changes for Lucy**
1. Tighten support-copy contrast and spacing rhythm.
2. Keep motion to scroll reveals and button press feedback only.
3. Preserve the image-first structure and avoid adding decorative elements.
