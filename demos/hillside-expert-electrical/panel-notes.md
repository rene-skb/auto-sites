# Panel notes — Hillside Expert Electrical

## Creative direction panel

- **Steve Jobs:** The strongest idea is inspection readiness. Make the page feel like competent electrical work, not lifestyle remodeling. Use confidence, paperwork, and clean sequencing as the brand.
- **Paul Graham:** The facts are good. Cut anything that sounds like research notes. Say what they do, where they do it, what proof exists, and how to call.
- **David Ogilvy:** Lead with the inspection promise. License #1052290 and the phone number are strong direct-response proof. Keep them close to the hero.

Direction: a permit-file electrical page with premium craft, sharp proof, and two user paths: call for service, upload plans for builder bids.

## WHY critique

### Steve Jobs — 8.1
- The big headline and file-card proof feel like a real point of view.
- The initial “power work” phrasing is clever but unnatural. Make it plain and inevitable.
- The logo/name mismatch undercuts the authority created by the rest of the page.

### Paul Graham — 7.9
- The structure is clear and specific.
- A few lines still reveal the research process: “the current site lists,” “the site says.” Strip those out.
- The kitchen hero can work if the caption anchors it to electrical scope, not remodeling taste.

### David Ogilvy — 8.0
- License number, free quotes, plan uploads, and phone CTA are useful proof.
- The strongest hook is “passes inspection.” Keep that language.
- The footer should not say “demo redesign” if the page is trying to feel client-ready.

**WHY panel average:** 8.00

**Changes for v1**
1. Rewrite the hero headline to “Electrical work built to pass inspection.”
2. Replace the general-construction logo crop with a consistent Hillside Expert Electrical wordmark.
3. Remove source/audit language from customer-facing copy.
4. Tighten the hero image card and make the caption explicitly electrical.

## WHAT critique

### Don Norman — 8.2
- The page quickly communicates code-compliant electrical work, but the rebuild section arrives too early and blurs the category.
- Proof should read as company confidence, not as an evidence report.
- The two audience paths are good: homeowners call, builders upload plans.

### Steve Krug — 8.1
- “26” is not scannable without reading the small text. Label the proof cards with their meaning.
- Use the same CTA words repeatedly. Too many verbs create small decisions that don’t need to exist.
- Project images need captions so people understand why they’re there.

### Jakob Nielsen — 7.9
- The information architecture should put primary services and license proof before special rebuild messaging.
- Navigation labels should match user tasks: services, rebuild support, license and proof, get a quote.
- Remove visible provenance phrases such as “official site says” and “no generic map link.”

**WHAT panel average:** 8.07

**Changes for v2**
1. Position Hillside as an electrical contractor first, with rebuild support as a specific secondary use case.
2. Standardize CTAs around call, request a quote, and upload plans.
3. Move proof and work examples above the rebuild section.
4. Add captions to project images and relabel the hero proof cards.

## HOW critique

### Massimo Vignelli — 8.2
- The system has strong discipline: cream, navy, red, heavy type, and one clear proof language.
- The hero card is overbuilt for its content. Reduce the volume and let the grid breathe evenly.
- The gallery needs a heading to become an intentional chapter rather than a pasted image block.

### Erik Spiekermann — 8.1
- The display face is confident, but section headings should step down after the hero.
- The wordmark is cleaner than the raster logo for this demo, and the nav feels credible.
- Body contrast on dark sections needs a touch more brightness.

### Dieter Rams — 8.3
- The design is useful and mostly restrained.
- Remove excess vertical air before it becomes decoration.
- Do not add electrical gimmicks. The proof, phone number, and license are the design.

### Emil Kowalski — 8.2
- Keep the motion simple: fade-up sections, stagger cards, active button scale, no parallax.
- Avoid hover zoom on static images per Scott’s rules.
- Use motion to confirm structure, not to perform electricity.

**HOW panel average:** 8.20

**Changes for v3**
1. Tighten hero card height, section padding, and button shadows.
2. Add a work-examples section heading and captions.
3. Reduce repeated heading scale after the hero.
4. Keep motion restrained and compliant with reduced-motion settings.
