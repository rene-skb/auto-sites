# Panel Notes — Shafran Construction Inc.

## WHY critique

### Steve Jobs — 8.1
- The strongest idea is the estimate desk: practical construction work starts before demolition.
- The hero should feel calm and prepared, not like a generic contractor brochure.
- Keep the official completed-project image as the trust anchor.

### Paul Graham — 7.8
- The service breadth is real but easy to blur. Build, renovate, repair, upgrade should become the organizing system.
- Cut any rescue-language about the old site. The visitor only cares whether Shafran can handle the job.
- Avoid broad claims like “highest quality” unless the source says it and it earns the space.

### David Ogilvy — 8.0
- “Plan the work before the wall opens” is specific enough to make the homeowner think about risk.
- The phone CTA is stronger when paired with what to prepare before calling.
- License #547455 should sit near the conversion path, not hide in a badge.

**Panel average:** 7.97

**Next changes**
1. Tighten the hero around estimate readiness.
2. Use the four service modes as the main page structure.
3. Attach “licensed” language to CSLB Lic# 547455 wherever it appears.
4. Keep copy concrete and sourced.

## WHAT critique

### Don Norman — 8.2
- The page now gives the visitor a clear first choice: build, renovate, repair, or upgrade.
- The quote-prep section reduces anxiety by saying what to bring to the first call.
- The plain office/address block works better than an unverified map link.

### Steve Krug — 8.0
- The header, hero CTAs, service cards, and final contact block are easy to scan.
- Remove any repeated full contact detail blocks. One quick proof line plus the final contact card is enough.
- The fire-damage image should be clearly labeled so it doesn’t feel like the main brand promise.

### Jakob Nielsen — 7.9
- Contrast is strong on the dark chapter and CTA card.
- Mobile stacking should preserve the service order and not trap the phone number below too much copy.
- The license proof is useful, but the image badge should not be the only accessible statement.

**Panel average:** 8.03

**Next changes**
1. Keep one final contact card with phone, email, office, and license.
2. Remove generic map links because no canonical business listing URL was verified.
3. Label the official project photos by job type.
4. Check mobile ordering for CTAs and service cards.

## HOW critique

### Massimo Vignelli — 8.2
- The grid is coherent: hero, service matrix, dark work chapter, decision chapter, close.
- The square official images fit the layout instead of being forced into a wide crop.
- The dark section creates a distinct chapter without adding visual clutter.

### Erik Spiekermann — 8.1
- Libre Baskerville plus Epilogue gives enough authority without defaulting to another contractor sans pair.
- Body sizes and line-height are readable, especially on dark sections.
- Keep display serif limited to big statements and avoid decorative review-style text.

### Dieter Rams — 8.2
- The page uses fewer objects than the current site and makes the useful facts easier to find.
- No stats bar. The years and license proof are woven into the page where they matter.
- Remove anything that smells like “proof for proof’s sake.” Keep the proof boring and checkable.

### Emil Kowalski — 8.3
- Motion should be scroll reveal only, with stagger on service cards, project cards, and prep steps.
- Button active states should be tactile and fast.
- No image hover motion. Project photos are evidence, not interactive toys.

**Panel average:** 8.20

**Next changes**
1. Add standard scroll reveals with reduced-motion support.
2. Preserve still images with no hover scale.
3. Keep section rhythm on an 8px-derived scale.
4. Run source-language, em dash, and image-path checks before deploy.
