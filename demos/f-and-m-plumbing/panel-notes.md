# Panel notes — F & M Plumbing

## WHY critique

### Steve Jobs — 8.0
- The hero has a real point of view: urgent water problems need the phone call first.
- The dark copper palette feels specific to plumbing without becoming a cartoon of pipes and wrenches.
- Remove the internal redesign language. Customers should never see the scaffolding.

### Paul Graham — 7.6
- The page is clear because it sticks to the useful facts: service line, hours, address, service scope.
- Some copy still talks about the old website instead of talking to the homeowner with a problem.
- The map area looks broken in the screenshot. If the embed can’t render cleanly, design around it.

### David Ogilvy — 8.2
- “When water needs to stop right now” is specific and easy to remember.
- The phone CTA is correctly repeated in high-intent contexts.
- Review cards should keep the actual customer details but lose any source-process explanation.

**WHY average:** 7.93

**Changes for v1**
1. Strip all internal/demo/source-process language from customer-facing copy.
2. Replace the headless-broken map block with a designed contact/service-area panel and keep the official contact link.
3. Tighten the intro around the customer problem rather than the redesign critique.
4. Keep the hero conviction and copper/navy direction.

## WHAT critique

### Don Norman — 7.9
- The page makes the core action obvious: call the plumber.
- A few headings are too editorial for a stressed homeowner scanning on a phone.
- Process and contact copy should speak directly to the customer, not cite where the fact came from.

### Steve Krug — 8.0
- Services are scannable, and repeated phone CTAs reduce decision friction.
- “Emergency path,” “site review,” and “Open F & M contact page” feel like labels from the build process.
- Keep the hero line, but make “24/7 Pasadena plumber” visible earlier for search-intent clarity.

### Jakob Nielsen — 7.7
- Information scent is strong: services, reviews, contact, service area.
- Mobile risk is headline height and long service cards.
- The contact section should put the phone and emergency service above decorative service-area content when stacked.

**WHAT average:** 7.87

**Changes for v2**
1. Add a plain “24/7 emergency plumber in Pasadena” line near the hero CTA.
2. Make service, process, review, and contact headings more literal.
3. Replace audit-style phrases with customer-facing service copy.
4. Change testimonial source labels to “Customer review.”

## HOW critique

### Massimo Vignelli — 8.0
- The navy, copper, and cream system is disciplined and category-appropriate.
- The giant type is strong, but the hero headline should give back a little space to the CTA.
- Keep the section grid simple; the confidence comes from proportion, not decoration.

### Erik Spiekermann — 8.1
- Barlow Condensed carries urgency well, and Public Sans keeps service copy readable.
- Some muted text is too soft for older homeowners in an urgent situation.
- Tighten the repeated giant-headline rhythm so scanning stays fast.

### Dieter Rams — 7.8
- The page does the useful job: call, understand services, check proof, contact.
- Remove any motion that delays access to information.
- Avoid decorative map tricks. The designed service-area panel is enough.

### Emil Kowalski — 8.0
- Motion should stay restrained: section reveals, hero line entrance, button active states.
- Add persistent mobile call access because emergency service is the core interaction.
- Do not animate or zoom static images. That would fight Scott’s non-negotiables.

**HOW average:** 7.98

**Changes for v3**
1. Reduce hero headline scale slightly and bring CTA utility higher.
2. Darken supporting copy for accessibility.
3. Tighten section padding and service-card density.
4. Add a mobile sticky call bar and keep motion restrained.
