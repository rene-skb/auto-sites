# Site Build Guidance — Scott's Review, March 23 2026

*Distilled from a full review of 40+ demo sites. Read this before every build.*

## Start Every Build Here
Before choosing fonts, colors, or layout: **"What makes THIS business different?"**
Choose typography, palette, and layout that only work for THIS business. A record shop should not look like a groomer. A food truck should not look like a tailor. The business personality drives the design, not a default template.

## Palette
- Three colors max: one dominant, one accent, one neutral.
- Extract colors from the business's actual branding (signage, Instagram, Google listing, van wrap) when possible.
- Warm text on warm backgrounds. Cool text on cool backgrounds. Don't mix temperatures (gray text on mustard = clash).
- Reference: Tarboosh (cream, terracotta, dark brown), Unicorn Sparkles (dark, gold, warm), King Koncrete (black, gold, gray).

## Typography
- Take risks. Let the business personality drive font selection. Don't default to the same safe pairing every time.
- The bigger the type, the fewer the words. At display scale you feel, you don't read.
- Consistency in craft quality, variety in creative choices across different builds.

## Layout
- Fewer containers, more space. When in doubt, remove the card and let content stand on its own. Elegance is restraint, not decoration.
- Whitespace separates sections. Lines interrupt. Use horizontal rules sparingly.
- Two-column layouts: headline and body text share a horizontal baseline.
- Centered text between left-aligned sections needs grounding (background shift, rules, or container).
- Test at three widths: 1440px desktop, 1024-1280px laptop, 375px mobile. Hero sections must reflow gracefully, not compress.

## Hero Sections
- Hero copy answers "why should I come here?" not the business origin story. Lead with value, vibe, or product.
- Strong heroes: "Already *worn in.*" / "Half the price. *Twice the care.*" / "BUILT TO OUTLAST EVERYTHING" / "Taste of *Cairo*"
- Weak heroes: founder ages, business history, long descriptions. Save those for the story section.
- Don't cram hero card + images + stats + CTAs into one contained box. Let them breathe.

## Images
- No fake storefronts, interiors, or building exteriors. Use product shots, textures, ingredients, mood photos, close-ups.
- Push for editorial, magazine-quality images. Close-up, shallow depth of field, warm lighting, textural detail. The prompt specificity controls output quality.
- For food businesses especially: photography IS the product. This is the single biggest quality lever.
- No reused images on the same page. Each image slot should be unique.
- Render at 2x minimum. Blurry images undermine everything.
- Verify image accuracy: don't show soft serve for a scoop shop, or Chinese food for a sushi restaurant.

## Social Proof & Reviews
- Pull real reviews from Google/Facebook/Reddit. This is a selling point: "Every review on the demo is real."
- Attribution format: first name + last initial + date + source. Best: "NEIL · MAY 2024 · Via Google." Also good: "MARK T., LANGFORD" (name + neighborhood for local businesses).
- All review cards same visual treatment. No alternating dark/light checkerboard. Content provides variety, not card colors.
- Press mentions (newspaper articles, blog features) should be styled differently from Google reviews — use a "PRESS" label or separate section.
- No duplicate quotes on the same page.

## Stats Bars
- Only include when you have 3-4 genuinely specific, verifiable data points that differentiate the business.
- Generic filler ("Locally Owned," "Quality Guaranteed") doesn't earn a stats bar. Leave it out and let the space breathe.
- Inline social proof (stats as plain text under body copy, no bar) can be more confident. Reference: Don Williams.
- Never stack two stats bars. One or zero.
- Placement doesn't have to be at the top. Contextual placement under an about section can work better.

## Maps & Contact Sections
- Pin to the specific business location, not a category search (which shows competitors).
- Suppress "No reviews" info windows. Load correct Google Place ID when possible.
- Balance column heights: map should match the height of the contact info beside it.
- Section headers like "Come Say Hello" or "Find Don" are warmer than "Location" or "Contact."

## Copy
- 2-3 sentences max per text block. Landing pages are not blog posts.
- CTAs with the actual phone number ("Call 250-721-0284") beat generic buttons ("Phone for Booking").
- Business name must be consistent throughout — header, body, footer. Don't add or change words.
- Avoid sensitive content (military, political) on business landing pages.

## Noise Texture
- Can add warmth to dark sites when done right. Current implementation is too coarse — visible dots instead of film grain.
- Must be very fine. If you can see individual particles, it's too heavy.

## Accent Lines
- Left vertical accent lines are overused. Use selectively, not as a default.
- Accent lines that span between two separate elements (header to stats bar) become cages. Keep them short and local.

## Decorative Elements
- Must earn their space. Small squares, floating shapes, heavy overlays — if they don't add meaning, remove them.

## Winning Patterns (Reuse and Adapt)
- **Split hero:** Text left, full-bleed image right that scales with page (Crow's Nest)
- **Full-viewport background hero:** Darkened photo + massive display type + one accent word + single CTA (King Koncrete, Los Panas)
- **Centered hero:** Serif + italic emphasis, info bar below, generous whitespace (Botched Vintage)
- **"Culture code" section:** Roman numerals or house rules, thin dividers, just type and space. Storytelling framework (Ditch Records "crate digger's code"). Transfers to any business.
- **Full-width colored quote:** Site palette background, italic serif, generous padding, personal attribution (Don Williams olive section, Tarboosh orange ribbon)
- **Inline social proof:** Stats as plain text under body copy, no bar (Don Williams)
- **Announcement bar:** Functional info at very top for businesses with variable locations/hours (Los Panas food truck)
- **3-2 rhythm:** Three-column grid followed by a two-image strip. Creates visual variety within consistent styling (Unicorn Sparkles)
- **Descriptive review attributions:** "RETURNING CLIENT — TATTOO" or name + neighborhood "MARK T., LANGFORD"

## Reference Sites (Study These)
1. **Unicorn Sparkles** — Overall benchmark. Photography + palette + pacing + consistency.
2. **Don Williams** — Best copy, most personality, inline social proof.
3. **Los Panas Kitchen** — Best food photography, announcement bar, cultural specificity.
4. **King Koncrete** — Best trades site. Consistent hero to footer.
5. **Tarboosh** — Palette discipline reference. Restraint IS sophistication.
6. **Crow's Nest Tattoo** — Artist showcase, split hero, storytelling.
7. **Ditch Records** — Bold typography, culture section, strongest CTA close.

## Critical Process Note
The review panel should refine the creative vision, not replace it. If a build starts with a bold, business-specific direction, the panel should make that direction better — not default it back to a safe template. More rounds ≠ better when feedback homogenizes the output. Protect distinctive choices.
