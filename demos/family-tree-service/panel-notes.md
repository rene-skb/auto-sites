# Panel notes - Family Tree Service

## Creative direction panel

### Steve Jobs
- Make the page feel like controlled risk: mature trees, steep lots, storm damage, and a crew that stays calm.
- Avoid the soft nature-brand cliché. This is service work near homes.
- Direction score: 8.0

### Paul Graham
- Say what they do, where, and why trust them. Cut brochure language.
- The strongest proof is license, insurance, free estimates, and 20+ years.
- Direction score: 7.7

### David Ogilvy
- The headline should sell relief from a specific homeowner problem.
- "Dangerous branches handled calmly" is specific enough to make the phone CTA make sense.
- Direction score: 8.1

## WHY critique

### Steve Jobs - 8.0
- The calm-risk idea lands. The page feels like a professional crew, not a lawn-care template.
- The hero image should feel finished and controlled rather than chaotic. The cleanup image is the right lead.

### Paul Graham - 7.7
- Clear, plain, and mostly cut down. Keep watching for phrases that explain the demo rather than the business.
- The service list is useful because it answers what they actually do before the page gets poetic.

### David Ogilvy - 8.1
- The headline is direct and human. It makes the phone number feel urgent without yelling.
- License and discounts in the hero board are strong proof. No need for a fake testimonial layer.

**WHY panel average:** 7.93

**Next changes**
1. Keep phone and estimate actions above the fold.
2. Make proof feel like operating facts, not a stats bar.
3. Use service cards to show breadth without turning the hero into a service dump.

## WHAT critique

### Don Norman - 8.0
- The main task is obvious: call or request an estimate.
- The service groupings are clear, and the proof ledger reduces decision anxiety.

### Steve Krug - 8.1
- Visitors can scan this fast. Pasadena, tree care, free estimates, phone number, license.
- The page correctly avoids a generic map link when a real business-pin URL was not confirmed.

### Jakob Nielsen - 7.8
- Contrast and type size are solid. Mobile navigation needs to stay simple and literal.
- Since hours are not confidently sourced, omitting them is better than inventing a schedule.

**WHAT panel average:** 7.97

**Next changes**
1. Keep contact facts in one final chapter and one hero CTA area, not scattered everywhere.
2. Do not add review cards without compliant names.
3. Avoid hours unless verified from a reliable source.

## HOW critique

### Massimo Vignelli - 8.0
- The centered-minimal hero gives the site a distinct rhythm from recent local-service builds.
- The four proof cells work because they sit inside the hero image board, but they must not become a separate stats strip.

### Erik Spiekermann - 8.1
- Source Serif 4 and Spline Sans suit the work: trustworthy but not sleepy.
- Body text is large enough and the dark section has enough line height.

### Dieter Rams - 7.9
- The page is mostly reduced to what matters. The generated images are acceptable as illustrative mood, but real job photography would raise the ceiling.
- No duplicate image use and no fake storefront/interior claims.

### Emil Kowalski - 8.0
- Motion should be limited to reveal, stagger, and button press feedback.
- No hover zoom on static images. Keep the site calm.

**HOW panel average:** 8.00

**Next changes**
1. Keep motion progressive and restrained.
2. Preserve no-hover image rule.
3. Run copy grep for source/demo language and em dashes before shipping.
