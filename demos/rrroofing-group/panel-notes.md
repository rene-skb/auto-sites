# Panel Notes — RRRoofing Group

## WHY critique

### Steve Jobs — 8.0
- The strongest idea is the moment a roof leak becomes a plan. Keep the page calm, not macho.
- The hero line lands because it names the homeowner fear without over-explaining.
- The first version let the proof card compete with the headline; reduce the metrics and keep the action clear.

### Paul Graham — 7.7
- The current site’s repeated SEO language should be cut down to the useful sequence: call, inspection, written estimate, work.
- Avoid source/audit wording in visible copy. Customers do not care that the copy came from a brief.
- Keep every claim traceable, especially license, insurance, emergency line, and no-subcontracting language.

### David Ogilvy — 8.1
- “Stop the leak before it spreads” is specific, urgent, and human.
- The best supporting copy is practical: inspection, photos, written estimate, cleanup.
- Do not use fake review language. If reviews are not verified, build proof from license and process facts.

**Panel average:** 7.93

**Concrete changes for v1**
1. Rework the hero proof card so it reads as a call checklist, not a stats strip.
2. Remove internal source/brief language from customer-facing copy.
3. Remove skeptical “claimed” labels and state only verified facts.
4. Swap repeated imagery so each photo appears once.

## WHAT critique

### Don Norman — 8.0
- Clear emotional promise and good action path.
- The user’s immediate next step should be more direct.
- Trust signals are present, but they need clearer labels.

### Steve Krug — 7.5
- The page is easy to understand, but the middle sections shout too evenly.
- Some process and trust copy repeats the same idea.
- Secondary CTAs should say exactly what happens.

### Jakob Nielsen — 7.0
- The phone CTA is visible, license appears, and the process is understandable.
- The “proof” section should not over-promise reviews or ratings we did not verify.
- Reduce friction by making the official estimate path obvious.

**Panel average:** 7.50

**Concrete changes for v2**
1. Reduce middle-section heading scale so the hero remains the loudest moment.
2. Rename sections for scanability: services, what happens after you call, proof you can check.
3. Point estimate CTAs to the official quote path instead of vague secondary language.
4. Reframe proof around verifiable license, insurance, own-crew, and written-estimate facts.

## HOW critique

### Massimo Vignelli — 8.4
- Strong modular grid and clear page order.
- Tighten the hero grid; the right card is slightly heavy.
- Standardize vertical rhythm between proof and contact.

### Erik Spiekermann — 8.1
- Barlow Condensed feels right for roofing: blunt, physical, and urgent.
- Hero headline is near saturation; reduce it slightly and improve leading.
- Body copy needs a touch more breathing room in dense cards.

### Dieter Rams — 8.2
- Useful structure: what they do, what happens, proof, how to call.
- Reduce repeated “written estimate” language.
- Keep only the strongest facts in the hero side card and add license continuity in the footer.

### Emil Kowalski — 8.2
- Visual system is cohesive and premium for a local trade.
- Add tactile hover/focus states for CTAs and links.
- Motion should be restrained: fade-up cards, quick hero entrance, no bounce.

**Panel average:** 8.23

**Concrete changes for v3/v4**
1. Slightly reduce hero headline size and side-card width.
2. Remove one hero-card row and soften location pills.
3. Add footer license number and tactile hover/focus states.
4. Keep scroll reveal restrained and respect reduced motion.
