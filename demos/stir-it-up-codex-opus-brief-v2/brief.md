# Creative Brief — Stir It Up, Victoria BC

*Written by Rene on Opus. This is the creative north star. Follow it.*

## The Business

**Stir It Up** — Caribbean soul food
- 760A Yates Street, Victoria BC (down an alley)
- Phone: real business, get number from workspace materials if needed
- 4.5★, 188 Google reviews
- Real Instagram photos available in workspace — use these, don't generate AI images
- No website currently

## Reality

This is not a chain. This is not a concept restaurant. This is a cook doing the food they know down an alley in Victoria. People who find it bring other people. That's the whole story.

The Google reviews aren't saying "amazing culinary experience." They're saying "I can't believe this is in a back alley in Victoria." "This is the best jerk chicken I've had since Toronto." "The owner is here. He cooks it himself." It's that kind of place.

## The Core Truth (Don't Ignore This)

**Stir It Up's power is that it doesn't need marketing.**

The worst thing we could do is build a site that tries to convince someone to come. That's not how this place works. People come because someone told them. The site should feel like the thing that person texts the link to — a confirmation, not a pitch.

So the brief isn't "sell the restaurant." The brief is: **"Be the proof that it's real."**

## Concept

**"Down the alley."**

That's the whole thing. The hiddenness is the point. The alley is the point. The signage painted on the wall is the point. You walk past it if you don't know, and once you know, you always know.

Everything on the page should honor that feeling. Not "hidden gem" energy (cliché). Not "local favorite" energy (generic). Just: *you found it, and now you know.*

## Tone

- **Plainspoken.** No adjectives stacked on adjectives. No "vibrant Caribbean flavors." Real cooks don't talk like that.
- **Confident without selling.** State facts. Let the food photos do the work.
- **Specific over evocative.** "Jerk chicken, curry goat, oxtail" beats "Caribbean classics."
- **Warm but not performative.** Like the cook wiping his hands on his apron before telling you what's good today.
- **Dry humor allowed.** The alley itself is inherently funny. Lean into it gently.

## What To Write, What Not To Write

### Write like this:
- "Caribbean soul food. Down the alley at 760A Yates."
- "Jerk chicken. Curry goat. Oxtail. Rice and peas."
- "188 people on Google. 4.5 stars."
- "Open for lunch, usually gone by mid-afternoon." (only if true)
- "It's in the alley. Keep walking."

### Do NOT write like this:
- "Stir It Up brings the vibrant flavors of the Caribbean to Victoria"
- "Experience authentic island cuisine"
- "A hidden gem you have to try"
- "The strongest proof of quality is..."
- "This section demonstrates..."
- Anything that sounds like a strategist explaining why the page works
- Anything that sounds like a review of its own copy

**Ban list** (literal phrases to avoid):
- "authentic"
- "vibrant"
- "hidden gem"
- "experience"
- "journey"
- "flavors of"
- "a taste of"
- "brings you"
- "destination"
- "reimagined"
- any phrase that starts with "Whether you're..."

## Visual Direction

### Hero
Lead with the alley or the food, not a logo. The hero should feel like the moment you realize the place is real — either the handpainted sign on the alley wall, or the food itself plated simply. **One strong image. One strong line. That's it.**

Headline direction (pick one or riff):
- "Down the alley. 760A Yates."
- "Caribbean soul food. In the alley."
- "You found it."

Don't use more than one line in the hero. No subheadings that explain the headline. The image does the explaining.

### Palette
Pull from the actual food and signage in the Instagram photos. Likely:
- deep warm browns (rice and peas, jerk chicken char)
- turmeric / curry gold as accent
- cream or parchment for body background
- maybe a green pulled from scotch bonnet / callaloo

Avoid: reggae flag primaries (red/yellow/green stripes), tiki kitsch, beach colors.

### Typography
- **Display:** something with warmth and slight character. A serif with soul, or a sturdy display sans with personality. Not elegant. Not corporate.
- **Body:** clean, readable, generous line height.
- **Not** the same DM Sans / Inter / Work Sans default everyone uses.

### Layout
- Editorial, not SaaS.
- Images should feel placed, not gridded.
- Avoid cards wherever possible. This is the Codex trap — resist it.
- Fewer sections, more breathing room per section.
- If a section feels like a "feature," delete it.

## Section Flow

Keep it ruthlessly short. This isn't a corporate site.

1. **Hero** — alley + food + one line. Practical CTA (Google Maps / directions).
2. **What they cook** — a list. Literally just the dishes, in the cook's language if possible. Let the food photos carry it.
3. **The place** — one paragraph about the alley, the cook, the vibe. Not origin story, not mission statement. Just what it is.
4. **What people say** — aggregate: 4.5 stars, 188 Google reviews. Link out to Google. Do NOT fabricate quotes. Do NOT use named reviewer placeholders.
5. **How to find it** — address, alley directions, phone, hours if known. Google Maps link. This is the most important practical section.
6. **Footer** — minimal.

That's it. No "Our Story." No "Why Choose Us." No "Testimonials" with fake quotes. No three-column "Features" grid.

## Photography Rules

- Use ONLY real Instagram photos from workspace.
- Crop aggressively if needed. Tight crops on food are stronger than wide restaurant shots.
- Owner presence is good if photos support it. Don't force it.
- No AI generation. If a photo slot doesn't have a real image, cut the slot.

## Copy Discipline (Codex-Specific)

Because this is being built on Codex with the Codex skill, pay extra attention:

- **Never let reasoning into copy.** No "the strongest proof," "the important thing," "you don't need more persuasion."
- **Never explain what the page is doing.** No meta.
- **Never reference the build process.** No "sourced from," "based on listing info," "verified via."
- **Write like a human who works at the restaurant**, not like a designer explaining the restaurant to another designer.
- Every single line of visible copy should pass this test: *Would the cook actually say this?*

If any line fails that test, cut it or rewrite it until it passes.

## Success Criteria

Someone from Victoria sees this site and texts it to their friend with just: "go here."

That's the whole goal.

If the site makes you want to drive to Yates Street, it worked. If it makes you feel like you just read a strategy memo about a restaurant, it failed.
