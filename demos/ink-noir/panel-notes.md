# Ink Noir — Panel Notes

## WHY critique

### Steve Jobs — 7.8
- The strongest idea is artist fit, not generic tattoo swagger. Keep that front and center.
- The page feels more inevitable when it talks directly to the booking decision instead of commenting on the old site.
- The black-heavy imagery supports the tone, but the headline has to feel cleaner than the explanation under it.

### Paul Graham — 7.4
- Cut anything that sounds like a review of the existing website. Say what this place does well.
- The useful claim is simple: you can figure out the right artist before you submit.
- Shorter section intros improve trust because they sound less produced.

### David Ogilvy — 8.1
- “Walk in knowing which artist fits the piece” is the right kind of headline, specific and useful.
- The proof section should stay factual, no chest beating, no fake customer romance.
- The booking CTA is literal and good. Keep it.

**WHY panel average:** 7.77

**Next changes**
- Tighten hero headline and body so the value lands in 1 pass.
- Remove meta-commentary about the current site.
- Make section intros shorter and more visitor-facing.

## WHAT critique

### Don Norman — 7.7
- The scan path is good once the hero is tightened: style, proof, visit.
- The page works because the practical information is visible early.
- Keep the routing steps short and decision-oriented.

### Steve Krug — 7.9
- It mostly passes the “don’t make me think” test.
- The useful links and hours section are clearer than most tattoo sites.
- The only risk is over-explaining why artist matching matters. Visitors already get it fast.

### Jakob Nielsen — 7.6
- Strong recognition over recall. Styles are named, artists are named, actions are obvious.
- Good use of direct labels and literal CTAs.
- Proof cards should stay visually uniform and concise.

**WHAT panel average:** 7.73

**Next changes**
- Shorten section intros and repeated explanations.
- Keep utility labels plain and immediate.
- Tighten proof card copy for faster scanning.

## HOW critique

### Massimo Vignelli — 8.0
- The centered hero with a portrait-led image block is the right system for this photo set.
- Typography carries enough weight, especially with the Bodoni + Space Grotesk contrast.
- The page feels like chapters rather than one long texture wash.

### Erik Spiekermann — 7.7
- The serif does the brand work, the sans keeps the facts readable.
- Good contrast and body size discipline on dark backgrounds.
- Keep the card copy tighter than you think you need.

### Dieter Rams — 7.5
- The best parts are the straightforward ones: style cards, proof cards, visit panel.
- Nothing crucial should feel ornamental.
- Leave the proof section restrained. The facts already do enough.

### Emil Kowalski — 7.8
- Reveal motion should stay quiet: opacity and translate only.
- Stagger cards, keep buttons responsive with scale(0.97), and respect reduced motion.
- No image zooms. The photography is static, so let the layout carry the polish.

**HOW panel average:** 7.75

**Next changes**
- Keep all card treatments uniform and reduce any decorative noise.
- Apply restrained reveal motion only to sections and cards.
- Preserve the portrait-led layout, do not force a wide hero.
