## What Makes Them Different

This isn't a yoga studio that happens to be hot. Heat IS the product. The room is the teacher. Everything — yoga, Pilates, sauna — runs through heat as a transformative medium. 714 people gave them 4.9 stars not because of fancy instructors or trendy playlists, but because they walked into a hot room and came out different.

The two location names tell the story: RINSE and RIO. RINSE is the ritual — sweat, cleanse, emerge. RIO is the energy — movement, rhythm, life. Together they frame heat not as a feature but as a philosophy.

Women-owned and LGBTQ+ friendly aren't checkboxes here. Halifax's yoga scene is small enough that these markers define who walks through the door. This is a space people chose because it chose them first.

## Creative Direction

### Concept: "The Room"
Everything points back to the room itself. The heat, the sweat, the silence after class. Not the instructors, not the philosophy, not the brand story. The room.

This is a site about a physical sensation you can't get from a screen. So the design should create tension — beautiful restraint that makes you want to feel what you can't see.

### Tone
- Declarative. Short sentences. Commands, not invitations.
- Physical language. Verbs that land in the body: drip, press, hold, breathe, rinse.
- Confident without being aggressive. This place doesn't sell. It states.
- Zero wellness jargon. No "journey," no "practice," no "mindful." Just what happens in the room.

### Hero Direction
Lead with heat. Not a person, not a pose — the room itself. Steam, glow, warmth. The headline should feel like the first breath when you open the studio door.

**Headline options (pick or riff):**
- "The room is already warm."
- "110°F. You'll know in the first minute."
- "Every class starts the same way. You open the door and the heat finds you."

Subline should ground it: what the place is, where it is. Let the headline carry the feeling, let the subline carry the fact.

### Color Palette
- **Primary:** Deep charcoal (#1a1a1a) — the room before the lights come up
- **Accent:** Warm amber/copper (#c47a3a) — heat made visible, sauna glow
- **Secondary:** Soft fog/steam (#e8e0d6) — the exhale, the cool-down
- **Text:** Off-white (#f5f0eb) on dark, charcoal on light sections

No red. No orange fitness energy. This is heat as stillness, not heat as intensity. Think sauna, not spin class.

### Typography
- **Display:** Something with weight and warmth. A condensed serif or a geometric sans with presence. Not delicate — this is a place where people sweat.
- **Body:** Clean, readable sans-serif. Generous line height. The body text should breathe like the spacing between poses.

### Section Flow

1. **Hero** — The room. Heat. One strong headline. CTA: "Find the schedule" or "Try a class."
2. **The concept** — What RSTUDIOS actually is. Hot yoga. Hot Pilates. Sauna. Two locations. Short, declarative paragraphs. No origin story.
3. **What happens** — Three offerings (Hot Yoga / Hot Pilates / Sauna), each in 2-3 sentences. Physical descriptions. What your body does, not what your mind achieves.
4. **Social proof** — 714 reviews at 4.9. Don't fabricate quotes. Use the aggregate as a statement: "714 people rated this 4.9 stars on Google." Let the number speak. Link to Google Maps.
5. **Who this is for** — Women-owned. LGBTQ+ friendly. Membership or drop-in. This section should feel like a door opening, not a sales pitch.
6. **Locations** — RINSE (1512 Dresden Row) and RIO (second Halifax location, address unconfirmed). Phone. Google Maps link for directions.
7. **CTA / Footer** — Call to book. Simple. One phone number. The rest is already in the room.

### What NOT To Do
- No fabricated review quotes or reviewer names
- No specific hours (not confirmed)
- No pricing or class schedule
- No founder backstory or origin story
- No "journey" or "transformation" language
- No image hover effects or parallax
- No stats bar
- No solo-operator language (this is a team/studio)

### Photography Direction (AI-Generated)
Since no real photos are available, generate images that feel:
- Warm, amber-lit. Steam and glow, not harsh studio lighting.
- Minimal people or silhouettes only. Focus on the space.
- Close-ups: water on skin, rolled mats, sauna wood grain, steam rising.
- No stock-photo yoga poses. No smiling-at-camera wellness shots.

## Success Criteria
The site should make someone who's never done hot yoga feel the heat through their screen. Physical, sensory, almost uncomfortable. Then offer the door in.
