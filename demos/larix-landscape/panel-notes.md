# Panel Notes — Larix Landscape

## Creative direction panel

### Steve Jobs
- Make the site feel like it understands Victoria weather, not just landscaping.
- The conviction is “outdoor space that survives rain, slope, and daily use.”
- Score: 8.1

### Paul Graham
- The current business has useful facts buried inside generic contractor copy. Cut the generic claims and keep the concrete service reality.
- The site should say drainage, walls, gardens, patios, and process in plain language.
- Score: 8.0

### David Ogilvy
- The strongest headline territory is the useful promise: a yard that looks good and handles water.
- Avoid inflated words like “top-shelf,” “exquisite,” and “dream backyard.”
- Score: 8.1

**Creative direction average:** 8.07

## WHY critique

### Steve Jobs
- The central idea lands: Victoria landscaping is really water, slope, and site management first.
- The headline is strong, but the hero image needs to carry the rain/site promise harder.
- Score: 8.0

### Paul Graham
- The page is clear and honest, especially the “manage water first” section.
- Remove internal/source-facing phrases like “the current site lists” and “this demo keeps.” They break the client-page illusion.
- Score: 8.2

### David Ogilvy
- “Built for rain. Made for outside.” is specific, local, and memorable.
- Add more human payoff: dry basement, usable patio, fewer soggy-yard headaches.
- Score: 7.9

**WHY panel average:** 8.03

**Changes for v1:**
1. Swap the hero to a stronger residential/dry-creek garden image.
2. Remove all visible “current site/demo/official source” language from customer-facing copy.
3. Tie more section copy back to the human payoff of Victoria-weather resilience.
4. Keep the water/site thesis as the spine and reduce competing slogans.

## WHAT critique

### Don Norman
- The water-first mental model is useful, but the practical service confirmation should be visible faster.
- CTA labels need one consistent action phrase.
- Score: 7.6

### Steve Krug
- The page tells a clean story, but mobile visitors may scroll too far before the next action.
- Add practical project chips and a compact CTA after the early service/problem section.
- Score: 7.4

### Jakob Nielsen
- Match to homeowner language is strong: wet corner, slope, pooling, foundation water.
- Standardize “Request a free estimate” and keep a persistent mobile action path.
- Score: 7.5

**WHAT panel average:** 7.50

**Changes for v2:**
1. Add hero chips that confirm common project types above the fold.
2. Standardize CTAs around “Request a free estimate.”
3. Add a compact early CTA strip after the water/problem cards.
4. Add a mobile sticky estimate/call bar and make project captions more outcome-specific.

## HOW critique

### Massimo Vignelli
- Strong modular discipline and clear section architecture.
- Hero card should align more rigorously with the headline block and card baselines should feel less approximate.
- Score: 8.4

### Erik Spiekermann
- Newsreader gives the brand confidence, but the display voice is used heavily.
- Light-section body text should be darker and slightly easier to read for working-client content.
- Score: 8.1

### Dieter Rams
- The page is practical and service-first without shouting.
- There are too many small pills/micro-elements in places. Consolidate repeated tags.
- Score: 8.6

### Emil Kowalski
- Color, borders, and honest local photos feel cohesive.
- Motion should stay quiet: section reveals, staggered cards, CTA active states. Avoid decorative parallax or image tricks.
- Score: 8.3

**HOW panel average:** 8.35

**Changes for v3:**
1. Darken body text on light sections and reduce a few repeated service chips.
2. Make project captions calmer with stronger contrast and breathing room.
3. Tighten hero card alignment and mobile headline sizing.
4. Carry restrained reveal/button motion into the motion pass without decorative effects.
