# Panel notes — RototillerGuy Landscaping

## WHY critique

### Steve Jobs — 8.0
- The central idea works: “Start with the soil” is specific, ownable, and much better than another lawn-service headline.
- The hero has too many proof objects. One strong finished-lawn image plus one process badge will land harder than a collage of four moments.
- The logo treatment weakens confidence. If the asset does not work on cream, use a clear text lockup.

### Paul Graham — 7.8
- The copy is mostly real and specific. The best sentence is the process sentence with pick-axe, rototill, compost, then till again.
- The page still carries some marketing shape from the source, especially in repeated CTA language and the demo footer note.
- Cut anything that sounds like provenance or inside baseball from visible copy.

### David Ogilvy — 8.1
- The headline has a reason to exist. The concrete method sells the service without hype.
- The review section needs a stronger proof cue. Add 5-star/source context without turning it into a stats bar.
- “Request a free bid” is the right action. Keep it near the process proof.

**WHY average:** 7.97

**Changes for v1**
1. Simplify the hero collage into one dominant finished-lawn image, one process image, and a compact method badge.
2. Replace the hard-to-read logo image in the header with a crisp text lockup plus keep the image in footer only if readable.
3. Remove demo/provenance language from visible footer copy.
4. Add lightweight rating/source cues to review cards while keeping every card the same treatment.

## WHAT critique

### Don Norman — 8.0
- The process cards are clear and the before/after proof helps people understand the work quickly.
- The hero needs a faster plain-language line: lawn installation, sod, sprinklers, rototilling, Los Angeles County.
- Phone and free-bid paths should stay visible and consistent.

### Steve Krug — 7.7
- Nobody should have to infer the service from “soil.” Keep the headline, but put the category right under it.
- Reduce the scroll tax. The page feels longer than the amount of content requires.
- Add short labels to service cards so visitors can scan before reading.

### Jakob Nielsen — 7.8
- Contrast fails on the secondary button inside the dark contact card.
- Review cards mention Google/Yelp but do not make the proof actionable enough.
- Footer logo/image treatment is inconsistent with the header.

**WHAT average:** 7.83

**Changes for v2**
1. Add a clear service/location line directly under the hero headline.
2. Tighten section spacing and make CTA language consistent around free bids and phone calls.
3. Add scannable service tags and before/after captions.
4. Fix dark-section secondary button contrast and replace footer logo image with the same text lockup.

## HOW critique

### Massimo Vignelli — 8.0
- The system has a strong idea, but the hero contains one layer too many.
- Remove the quick proof cards. Let the headline, service chips, CTA, and image do the work.
- Keep the grid quieter and more architectural.

### Erik Spiekermann — 7.9
- Barlow Condensed gives the page character, but it is overused at the card level.
- Use the display face for major statements and make smaller headings less shouty.
- Narrow the text measure in split sections.

### Dieter Rams — 8.1
- The soil image in the hero is decorative now. Remove it.
- The before/after proof and method cards earn their space; the hero proof cards do not.
- Footer contrast needs fixing.

### Emil Kowalski — 8.0
- Motion should feel sturdy: section reveals, staggered cards, button feedback, no bounce.
- Reveal the hero image and badge slightly after the headline so the page feels assembled, not animated for show.
- Add hover feedback only to real interactive elements: nav, CTAs, phone tiles, proof links.

**HOW average:** 8.00

**Changes for v3**
1. Remove the hero quick-card row and the decorative soil image from the hero.
2. Reduce display-type overuse inside smaller cards and narrow body measure.
3. Make footer branding readable.
4. Carry Emil’s restrained motion plan into v4.
