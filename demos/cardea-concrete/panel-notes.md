# Cardea Concrete - Panel Notes

## WHY critique

**Panel input:** `screenshot-v0.png` + `index-v0.html`

### Steve Jobs - 8.0
- The direction has conviction: heavy type, material palette, and real surface photography feel right for concrete.
- Hero line “Floors with weight” is handsome but slightly poetic for a high-intent contractor visitor. Make the promise more immediate.
- The proof section’s “spec sheet, not brochure” idea is strong. Bring that discipline higher.

### Paul Graham - 7.7
- The page identifies a real gap in the current site: too much generic contractor copy, not enough decision structure.
- It needs to answer the nervous homeowner’s question faster: “Do they do my exact surface?”
- The three paths work because they separate residential, commercial, and industrial without turning into SEO sludge.

### David Ogilvy - 8.1
- The page sells the thing people buy: a floor that can take abuse and still look intentional.
- CTA language should become more concrete. “Email for a quote” is fine, but “Send project photos” is better.
- Avoid license language unless the exact number is verified. Lead with verifiable contact and scope instead.

**WHY average:** 7.93

**Next changes**
1. Change hero headline to a clearer outcome: “Concrete floors that work hard and look finished.”
2. Add a small above-fold project checklist without making it a stats bar.
3. Rename email CTA to “Send project photos.”
4. Move the spec-sheet idea into the process language.

## WHAT critique

**Panel input:** `screenshot-v1.png` + `index-v1.html`

### Don Norman - 8.1
- The service paths are understandable and map to real visitor intent.
- The process section is useful, but “what you need the floor to handle” should appear earlier.
- The quote block should tell people what happens when they call.

### Steve Krug - 8.0
- The page is scannable. Big section heads do not hide the practical details.
- Navigation is minimal and clear.
- The gallery is attractive, but the page needs stronger labels around the photos so the images are not just ambience.

### Jakob Nielsen - 7.8
- Contrast is generally strong. Small caption text in the hero could be slightly larger.
- The four service cards are readable, but equal dark cards create a heavy band. Add one lighter “surface prep” treatment or more spacing.
- Contact details are easy to find, which is the most important usability outcome.

**WHAT average:** 7.97

**Next changes**
1. Add “surface, traffic, finish, timeline” as practical prep language in the hero support copy, not as metrics.
2. Give the gallery captions and reduce its purely decorative feel.
3. Lighten the service band by turning service cards into alternating material tiles.
4. Add call expectation copy in the contact section.

## HOW critique

**Panel input:** `screenshot-v2.png` + `index-v2.html`

### Massimo Vignelli - 8.2
- The grid has discipline and the concrete palette is restrained.
- The strongest system is the contrast between architectural serif headlines and utilitarian Archivo details.
- Remove anything that looks like a marketing badge. This page is better when it behaves like a project binder.

### Erik Spiekermann - 8.0
- Type hierarchy is solid. The display face should stay in headlines only.
- Body copy line-height is comfortable and the contact card is legible.
- Hero caption and fine print need a touch more size and opacity.

### Dieter Rams - 8.3
- The page is useful because it reduces the service list to decisions.
- Avoid decoration in motion. Reveal sections, not every tiny card.
- The final contact area should be the quietest, most practical part of the page.

### Emil Kowalski - 8.2
- Motion should feel like construction sequencing: large surfaces settle first, details follow.
- Use IntersectionObserver reveals with short stagger on cards and steps.
- Add button press feedback, respect reduced motion, and do not animate images with hover zoom.

**HOW average:** 8.18

**Next changes**
1. Add restrained scroll reveal with progressive enhancement only.
2. Increase small caption/fine-print readability.
3. Keep images static on hover, no zoom.
4. Make final contact area more operational and less promotional.
