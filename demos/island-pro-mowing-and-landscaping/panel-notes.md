# Panel Notes — Island Pro Mowing and Landscaping

## Creative direction

- Business: Island Pro Mowing and Landscaping Inc., Sidney-based lawn maintenance and landscaping contractor serving Greater Victoria and the Saanich Peninsula.
- Source-backed proof: Cam McLennan is president and coordinates daily operations/projects; site lists 15+ years local experience, residential/commercial clients, strata and estate maintenance, full-service lawn care and landscaping, phone `250-812-4676`, and an estimate form.
- Direction: practical Victoria property-care page. Make the first decision simple: call about maintenance, cleanup, or installation. Use official photos, earthy Vancouver Island greens/cream, and a phone-first quote path.
- Guardrails: no stats bar, no fabricated license/insurance claims, no fake project photos, no duplicate image use, no hover scale on static images, no process/source language in rendered copy.

## WHY critique

### Steve Jobs — 8.2
- “Keep the property looked after” is clear and human. It says why a homeowner, realtor, or strata manager should care.
- The hero works because it avoids a menu dump and turns the service into an outcome.
- The page could have become another contractor brochure, but the Cam coordination proof gives it a person.

### Paul Graham — 7.9
- The strongest idea is that Island Pro solves three real jobs: maintain, clean up, build.
- Good restraint on not inventing a grand story. It sticks to the facts.
- The one risk is breadth. Keep the service grouping tight so it does not feel like a directory.

### David Ogilvy — 8.2
- The phone number appears at the moments where intent is highest.
- The realtor quote is useful because it names the customer situation: work before a sale.
- “Call Cam, or send the project details” is plain and likely to pull better than a softer contact line.

**WHY average:** 8.10

**Changes for v1**
1. Keep the hero focused on property upkeep, not company background.
2. Move Cam/project coordination proof into the first major section.
3. Split residential/listing projects from strata/commercial maintenance.
4. Let the two strongest customer quotes carry trust without adding extra claims.

## WHAT critique

### Don Norman — 8.0
- The service rows map well to customer intent: lawn care, cleanup, install, build.
- Two pathways help different audiences choose without reading the entire page.
- The mobile call button is useful for a local service business.

### Steve Krug — 8.1
- The page scans quickly. I can tell what they do, where they work, and how to contact them.
- “Request a home estimate” and “Call about regular maintenance” are specific enough.
- The work images support the service sections instead of decorating them.

### Jakob Nielsen — 8.0
- Contact details are complete and visible: phone, president, address, service area, form.
- The service-area chips are useful, but the page should avoid repeating all logistics elsewhere.
- Review attribution follows the required first-name/last-initial pattern.

**WHAT average:** 8.03

**Changes for v2**
1. Add a fixed mobile call action.
2. Keep full logistics in the contact section only.
3. Use the estimate form for planned work and phone for recurring maintenance.
4. Caption project images with trade/scope labels.

## HOW critique

### Massimo Vignelli — 8.2
- The palette is disciplined: forest, cream, moss, clay. It feels regional without becoming decorative.
- The grid has strong order and the work-gallery columns end cleanly.
- The quote section is strong because it is open and typographic, not a checkerboard of cards.

### Erik Spiekermann — 8.1
- Fraunces gives the page a landscape/editorial tone, while Archivo keeps service copy sturdy.
- Tight letter spacing needed adjustment in the hero, but the final version reads cleanly.
- Body size and contrast are acceptable across the dark and cream sections.

### Dieter Rams — 8.3
- No stats bar, no fake proof, no extra widgets. The page says enough and stops.
- Official images are used once each. Static photos stay static.
- The contact section is practical and does not overcomplicate the estimate path.

### Emil Kowalski — 8.1
- Motion is restrained: scroll reveal, stagger, button active state, reduced-motion support.
- No image hover scaling. Good.
- The sticky mobile call action is functional, not theatrical.

**HOW average:** 8.18

**Changes for v3**
1. Tune headline letter spacing after screenshot review.
2. Remove source/provenance phrasing from rendered copy.
3. Prevent bottom cards from stretching into empty space.
4. Keep motion limited to reveal and button feedback.

## Final scores

- WHY: 8.10
- WHAT: 8.03
- HOW: 8.18
- Panel average: 8.10
- Self-review: 8.6

## Self-review

Strong fit for a local Victoria landscaping lead. The page is materially clearer than the source site: it creates audience paths, surfaces Cam as the accountable operator, uses real project photos, and gives phone/form actions without bloating the page. Main weakness is source photo quality. The official assets are real but older and soft, so the design leans on type, spacing, and color to compensate.
